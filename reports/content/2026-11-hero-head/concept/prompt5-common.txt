Character concept turnaround sheet for a dark medieval fantasy game: the head, neck and top of the shoulders of one man, shown four times side by side on a plain mid-grey background (#7a7c80): front view, three-quarter view (facing left), left profile, and back of the head. Same man, same scale and eye height in all four views. Soft even studio light from the upper front-left, no rim light. Rendered like a high-end realistic 3D game character sculpt (real skin, real hair strands), not a painting. Wide landscape image, about 3:1.

Two reference images:
- man.png — the previous concept sheet of this character. This is the man: keep his identity, his broad square face, his age, his weathered tanned skin with grime, his greasy olive-brown hair with golden strands combed back, the short strands standing up at the hairline, the short ponytail tied with a leather thong, the goatee, the torn linen collar, the layout and the four views.
- original.png — the face texture of the original 2002 game character (unwrapped, so it looks wider than the face is). Only to check that he still reads as this man.

The problem with man.png: the face is a mirror image of itself, left and right halves identical, so he looks like a mannequin and not like a living person. Redo him as a real man with a naturally uneven face. No real face is symmetric. Build the two halves of the face differently on purpose:
1. Eyes: his left eye is a little narrower than the right, its upper lid heavier and lower; the eyes are not at exactly the same height.
2. Eyebrows: the right eyebrow sits higher and is more arched, the left one is lower and straighter; they differ in thickness, and one has a small gap in the hairs.
3. Nose: broken once long ago and healed slightly crooked — the bridge leans a little to his left, one nostril is a little wider than the other.
4. Mouth: the mouth is slightly off the centre line; one corner sits lower than the other, the lips are not mirrored.
5. Beard: the goatee is cut unevenly with a knife, fuller on one side of the chin than the other; the moustache halves differ in length; the stubble along the jaw is patchy and heavier on one side.
6. Hairline and hair: the hairline is higher at one temple than the other; the short strands at the forehead fall to one side, not as a centred tuft; the hair strands lie differently on the left and the right.
7. Ears: one ear stands out a little more than the other.
8. Jaw and cheeks: one cheek a little fuller, the jaw line slightly different on each side.
9. Skin: tan, redness, grime and small marks are spread unevenly — a sunburnt patch on one cheekbone, a small mole on one cheek, a few pores and blemishes, dirt heavier on one side of the neck. No scars.
In the front view the head is turned and tilted by a couple of degrees, as a living person holds it, not locked like a passport photo. The asymmetry must be clearly visible in the front view when the left half is compared with the right, but it must stay natural: an ordinary man's face, not a deformed or injured one.

Also fix: the eyes are light grey-blue with a dark rim, not brown. He is 30 to 33, not older: keep the weathering, but no deep folds.

Avoid: a mirrored face, a centred tuft of hair, identical eyebrows, a perfectly straight nose, a doll or mannequin look, a wax figure, a handsome model face, a caricature, a visibly deformed face, a scowl, scars, tattoos, earrings, brown eyes, bright blue eyes, a full beard, a long ponytail, glossy wet skin, orange colour cast, painterly brush strokes.
