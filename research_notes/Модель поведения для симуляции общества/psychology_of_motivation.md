# Psychological and cognitive models of motivation that can be turned into computation for game NPCs (state of knowledge, 2026-11-04)

Evidence-quality tags used below: **[full text]** = I extracted and read the paper/page text; **[abstract/snippet]** = only an abstract or search-result summary was available; **[wiki/secondary]** = Wikipedia, wiki, blog or press release. "Inference" items are my own reasoning, not claims of the sources.

## 1. Maslow's hierarchy: what the evidence says, and how games and agent simulations use it

### Takeaway
The part of Maslow that survives is "several distinct, universal need systems, each of which adds to well-being when met". The part that does not survive is the strict stage model (a lower need must be met before a higher one even appears). The defensible versions are Maslow's own "soft" reading and Kenrick et al.'s 2010 revision: overlapping, context-triggered motives whose priority shifts with threat and opportunity.

### Cited Findings
- Kenrick et al. 2010 summarise Maslow's theory as two ideas: (a) several independent fundamental motivational systems, and (b) a hierarchy in which some motives have priority. They keep the foundation but "buttress" it with evolutionary theory. [full text] — [Kenrick, Griskevicius, Neuberg & Schaller 2010, Perspect. Psychol. Sci. 5(3):292-314](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/KenrickGriskeviciusNeubergSchaller2010.pdf)
- Kenrick et al.: the revised list has immediate physiological needs, self-protection, affiliation, status/esteem, and three reproductive goals (mate acquisition, mate retention, parenting). Later-developing goals are drawn as **overlapping** with earlier ones, not replacing them: "early developing motives are unlikely to be fully replaced by later goals but instead continue to be important throughout life". [full text] — [Kenrick et al. 2010](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/KenrickGriskeviciusNeubergSchaller2010.pdf)
- Kenrick et al.: developmental priority and "cognitive" (momentary) priority need not coincide; the cognitive hierarchy "might change dynamically with context at any point in an individual's life". Hunger and sexual desire can be suppressed by social/esteem concerns even when salient. Motivational systems are activated by proximate cues (their Table 1 lists triggers: food smell, darkness, unfamiliar angry males, etc.) and chronic priorities differ between people. [full text] — [Kenrick et al. 2010](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/KenrickGriskeviciusNeubergSchaller2010.pdf)
- Wahba & Bridwell 1976 (Organizational Behavior and Human Performance 15:212-240) reviewed factor-analytic and ranking studies: factor analyses often fail to recover Maslow's five categories (needs load on several factors), there is "no consistent support" for the proposed ordering, and rank-ordering is an inappropriate test method. One summary counts ten factor-analytic and three ranking studies with only partial support. I could not open the paper itself. [abstract/snippet, secondary quotes] — [Scilit record](https://scilit.net/publications/b729a8a400224bf9281a571f8efd7816); [Wikipedia: Maslow's hierarchy of needs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maslow%27s_hierarchy_of_needs) (says they found "little evidence" for the specific ranking, and blamed partly poor measurement and weak control groups) [wiki/secondary]
- Maslow himself said a need need not be fully satisfied before the next emerges; he described "decreasing percentages of satisfaction as we go up the hierarchy of prepotency". [wiki/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Maslow's hierarchy of needs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maslow%27s_hierarchy_of_needs). Tay & Diener (below) also quote Maslow as calling the order "soft" or "instinctoid" (influences but does not determine behaviour). [full text]
- Tay & Diener 2011 (J. Personality and Social Psychology 101:354-365), Gallup World Poll, **123 countries**, six need classes (basic, safety, social, respect, mastery, autonomy). Need fulfilment was consistently associated with subjective well-being (SWB) across world regions. Life evaluation was most associated with basic needs; positive feelings with social and respect needs; negative feelings with basic, respect and autonomy needs. [full text] — [Tay & Diener 2011 PDF](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)
- Tay & Diener: the associations of SWB with each specific need were "largely independent of whether other needs were fulfilled". Interaction terms between needs added little (country-level interactions about 2% of variance versus about 39% for the main effects of need fulfilment, for life evaluation). Results held when household income was controlled (income added 0-1% once needs were in the model). [full text] — [Tay & Diener 2011](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)
- Tay & Diener ordering test (multilevel item-response theory): basic and safety needs were the most likely to be fulfilled at low overall fulfilment, but with gradual slopes and **crossovers**; people with high overall fulfilment sometimes had psychological needs met before basic/safety ones. Among psychosocial needs the order was fairly consistent across countries (social, then autonomy, then mastery last); whether basic and safety needs were met depended strongly on country. They conclude there is "a tendency, but not a strong one, to fulfill the needs in a specific order". Also: it helps one's SWB if others in the nation have their needs met, beyond one's own fulfilment. [full text] — [Tay & Diener 2011](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)
- Cross-cultural / age critiques: Hofstede (1984) called the ordering ethnocentric; Tang & West (1997) found two need levels in peacetime and three in wartime for US respondents (different pattern for Egypt/Saudi Arabia); an Asian ordering study ranked "community" highest; a 1981 age study found esteem peaking in adolescence and security in old age. [wiki/secondary, primary studies not read] — [Wikipedia: Maslow's hierarchy of needs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maslow%27s_hierarchy_of_needs)
- Use in agent simulations: Humanoid Agents define "basic needs" citing Maslow 1943 (fullness, fun, health, social, energy) and justify their results as supporting Maslow ("health, energy and fullness grouped as low-level physiological and safety needs"). [full text] — [Wang, Chiu & Chiu, EMNLP 2023 demo](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-demo.15.pdf)
- AgentSociety (Piao et al., Tsinghua, 2025) gives LLM agents a three-part mind (emotions, needs, cognition); needs follow Maslow's hierarchy ordered "by their relative urgency and importance", updated by active behaviours, passive external events and current psychological state, and drive an explicit "Need - Plan - Behavioral Sequence" chain. The text I could read gives no need names, decay rates or per-need validation numbers. [full text, first half only] — [AgentSociety arXiv 2502.08691](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08691v1)
- Tay & Diener's reference list points to further empirical tests I did not read: Hagerty 1999 (national quality of life across time), Wicker et al. 1993 (deprivation/domination proposition), Rauschenberger et al. 1980 (Markov model of change in need strength). [full text of reference list only] — [Tay & Diener 2011](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Take Maslow" is defensible as a **list of need families** and as a **default tendency** (survival problems usually dominate), but not as a gate. A strict gate would starve higher-level behaviour (socialising, hobbies, status) in NPCs, and the human data show the independence of need contributions (Tay & Diener) — so additive/parallel need pressure is better supported than stage gating.
- The Kenrick view maps directly onto a computational design: each need has a **cue-triggered salience** (what is nearby, what threats exist) on top of an **internal deficit**, and the ranking is recomputed from context each decision, not fixed.
- Because cross-country data show basic/safety fulfilment depends on the society rather than the person, a society simulation should treat "can this NPC meet need X" as a function of property, job and settlement, not just personality.

### Gaps
- Hagerty 1999 and Taormina & Gao 2013 results were not retrieved (only named in references).
- The claim that the pyramid diagram was added by later authors (not Maslow) is known in the literature but I did not retrieve a source in this session; do not cite it from these notes.
- No source found that gives the exact "85/70/50/40/10 %" satisfaction figures that popular summaries attribute to Maslow; treat them as unverified.

## 2. Other motivation, values and personality models: defensibility, effect sizes, usefulness for computation

### Takeaway
SDT has the strongest empirical support for games (three needs independently predict enjoyment and continued play), but it models player motivation, and I found no evidence of SDT being used as an NPC need engine. Reiss, Max-Neef, Murray and Schwartz are descriptive taxonomies with much weaker or narrower validation. For personality, the Big Five/HEXACO give modest but real predictive power, and the key modelling fact is that behaviour varies hugely within a person, so traits should be parameters of distributions, not fixed switches.

### Cited Findings
**Self-Determination Theory (SDT)**
- Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006 found the needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness each independently predicted enjoyment and future game play (and compared SDT with Yee's motivation taxonomy). [abstract/snippet] — [Search result: Motivation and Emotion paper PDF](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf)
- Przybylski, Rigby & Ryan 2010 ("A Motivational Model of Video Game Engagement") argue the appeal and well-being effects of games stem from satisfying competence, autonomy and relatedness; competence is operationalised as the match between player skill and challenge. [abstract/snippet] — [Review of General Psychology, doi 10.1037/a0019440](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1037/a0019440)
- Tay & Diener's six needs include mastery and autonomy; autonomy and respect needs were linked to negative feelings, social and respect needs to positive feelings (see Section 1). [full text] — [Tay & Diener 2011](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)

**Reiss's 16 basic desires**
- Reiss and Havercamp started from 300+ statements about desires and used factor analysis on thousands of respondents; reports differ on whether the result was 15 or 16 desires (a 16th, "saving", reportedly emerged after about 3,500 more people). Reiss & Havercamp (1998) reported exploratory/confirmatory factor analyses and test-retest reliability. [abstract/snippet + press release] — [OSU news release](https://news.osu.edu/new-theory-of-motivation-lists-16-basic-desires-that-guide-us/); [search-result set on Reiss validation](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.562371/pdf)
- Independent support is partial: a UK web study by Aunger and colleagues, using its own set of 15 postulated motives, confirmed 14 of 15 by factor analysis (N about 500); a German replication (16 three-item scales, three studies, N = 1,568) was also reported. I found no strong independent methodological critique; one commercial blog claims the 16 desires were forced to be statistically orthogonal (unverified). [abstract/snippet, partly blog] — [search-result set](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.562371/pdf)

**Max-Neef**
- Nine fundamental needs (subsistence, protection, affection, understanding, participation, recreation, creation, identity, freedom) crossed with four existential categories (being, having, doing, interacting) = 36-cell matrix. Key distinction: **needs are constant across cultures, satisfiers vary**. Satisfier types: violators/destroyers, pseudo-satisfiers, inhibiting, singular and synergic (one satisfier serving several needs). No hierarchy, with subsistence the only exception; Max-Neef said the list is an example, not absolute or complete. [wiki/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Max-Neef's fundamental human needs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred_Max-Neef%27s_Fundamental_human_needs)

**Murray**
- Murray's *Explorations in Personality* (1938) introduced "needs" and "press" (environmental pressures); needs are manifest (overtly expressed) or latent; the system influenced later questionnaires such as the Personality Research Form; critics say the list is too broad and subjective. [wiki/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Psychological needs / Murray's system](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_needs)

**Schwartz values**
- Refined theory (Schwartz et al. 2012, JPSP 103:663-688): 19 values on a circular motivational continuum (adjacent values compatible, opposite values conflicting), refining the original 10; tested in 15 samples from 10 countries, N = 6,059; multidimensional scaling largely supported the predicted order, with a universalism/benevolence swap in one confirmatory test and support dependent on one instrument (the revised Portrait Values Questionnaire). [abstract/snippet] — [Refining the Theory of Basic Individual Values (record)](https://avesis.tedu.edu.tr/yayin/5030e84d-d374-42d4-a46c-b6f7b2d08457/refining-the-theory-of-basic-individual-values)

**Big Five / HEXACO and effect sizes**
- Roberts, Kuncel, Shiner, Caspi & Goldberg 2007 ("The Power of Personality", Perspect. Psychol. Sci. 2:313-345), restricted to prospective longitudinal studies: the effect of personality traits on mortality, divorce and occupational attainment was "indistinguishable" from that of socioeconomic status and cognitive ability. I did not retrieve the effect-size tables. [abstract/snippet] — [Roberts et al. 2007 record](https://stafforini.com/works/roberts-2007-power-personality-comparative/)
- Effect-size scale for psychology (Funder & Ozer 2019): r = .05 very small but may matter in the long run, .10 small, .20 medium and useful even in the short run, .30 large; effects above about .40 are unlikely and may signal overestimation. Cohen's older .1/.3/.5 is considered too generous. [abstract/snippet] — [Funder & Ozer 2019 (AMPPS)](https://uopsych.github.io/psy611/readings/Ozer_Funder_2019.pdf)
- Fleeson's Whole Trait Theory: traits are **density distributions of states**; weekly aggregates are stable (about .80-.90) but the majority of Big Five behaviour variance (estimates 62%-93%) is **within** persons, and a typical person routinely shows nearly all levels of all traits in daily life; the within-person variability is tied to situational cues, and people differ in the width of their distributions. [abstract/snippet] — [Fleeson & Law 2015 (zendy summary)](https://zendy.io/title/10.1037/a0039517); lifespan check: [Noftle & Fleeson, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2922931)
- HEXACO adds Honesty-Humility. Lee, Ashton & de Vries 2005 report HEXACO substantially outperforming the Five-Factor Model in predicting workplace delinquency in three countries. [abstract/snippet] — [Lee et al. 2005, Human Performance](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/2111765/Lee%20Human%20Performance%2018(2)%202005%20u.pdf). A 2019 Perspectives meta-meta-analysis (426 meta-analyses, 3,893 effect sizes) maps Honesty-Humility onto the outcome domain of **exploitation**, with each other HEXACO dimension tied to a distinct outcome domain. [abstract/snippet] — [Perspectives on Psychological Science 2019](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/1745691619895036/)
- Dwarf Fortress and The Sims 3 use trait systems (facets/traits) that change autonomous behaviour (see Section 4).

### Inferences
- For computation, the useful split is: **needs** = fast homeostatic variables (hours); **motives/values/personality** = slow parameters that set weights, thresholds, rates, and which satisfiers a character prefers. Max-Neef's needs-versus-satisfiers split is the cleanest design pattern for this: needs are few and universal, satisfiers (food type, tavern, fishing, prayer) are content that varies by culture, job, property.
- SDT variables are computable as slow running averages (competence = recent success rate against task difficulty; autonomy = share of recent actions chosen versus ordered/forced; relatedness = recent quality of contacts), but this mapping is a design proposal, not something the sources validated for NPCs.
- Personality effect sizes of r of about .2-.3 mean traits should shift behaviour **probabilities and thresholds**, not make behaviour deterministic; Fleeson's 62-93% within-person variance suggests also adding per-decision noise and situation-dependence so NPCs of the same "type" do not act identically.
- Honesty-Humility is the HEXACO dimension most relevant to a Gothic-style society (theft, bribery, exploitation of others), because it is the one linked to exploitation and delinquency outcomes.

### Gaps
- No effect sizes retrieved for SDT in games, for Big Five on specific everyday behaviours (eating, sleeping, socialising), or for Reiss/Schwartz predicting behaviour; all would need the primary papers.
- HEXACO effect sizes reported by the search tool (Honesty-Humility correlating -.44 with counterproductive work behaviour, .13 with citizenship, .15 with task performance, Lee, Berry & Gonzalez-Mule 2019) could not be mapped to a retrieved primary page; treat as unverified.
- Quantitative Reiss critique, independent Reiss replications beyond the two named, and Murray's measurement validity were not retrieved.
- HEXACO/Big Five effects on NPC-relevant daily-need rates (e.g. extraversion and social need decay) are not established; Humanoid Agents' authors only propose individual decay rates as future work.

## 3. Computational models: PSI / MicroPsi, homeostatic RL, drive reduction, intrinsic motivation, BDI, appraisal (OCC, EMA, FAtiMA)

### Takeaway
Two families are directly computable as state-variable-plus-update-rule models: Dörner/Bach's PSI (urges as deviations from targets, motive selection by urge strength times expectation, global modulators) and Keramati & Gutkin's homeostatic RL (reward = drop in a distance-from-setpoint "drive"). Both reduce to cheap vector arithmetic per NPC. BDI and appraisal models (OCC/EMA/FAtiMA) are more about structuring goals and emotions than about need dynamics, and their game results are thin.

### Cited Findings
**PSI theory / MicroPsi (Dörner, Bach)**
- Activity is directed at a finite set of pre-defined urges in three groups: physiological (food, water, physical integrity; relieved by consuming matching resources, raised by metabolism or damage), social (affiliation, raised by anti-legitimacy signals and fed by legitimacy signals), and cognitive (uncertainty reduction and competence, with task-specific and general parts; competence is frustrated by actual and anticipated failures). [wiki/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Psi-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi-theory)
- An urge signal arises when "a mismatch between a target value of a demand and the current value" occurs, with strength proportional to the deviation. A **motive** = drive + goal; several can be active but one determines behaviour, chosen using the urge strength and the anticipated probability of satisfying it; a **selection threshold** modulator controls how stable the dominant motive is against competitors. [wiki/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Psi-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi-theory)
- Global modulators: arousal (action readiness), selection threshold (stability of active goals), sampling/securing rate (rate of orientation behaviour), activation and resolution level (breadth/depth of perception, memory, planning). Emotion is described as a **configuration of modulator settings** plus pleasure/distress and urge assessment; ranges vary by individual. [wiki/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Psi-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi-theory)
- Implementations: MicroPsi (Java, 2003-2009), MicroPsi 2 (Python), OpenPsi in OpenCog (with robot interfaces). The emotion model was tested in simulation and fitted to human behavioural data, reproducing different personality types; the same page says most other predictions are qualitative and quantitative parameters are rarely supported by experiments. [wiki/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Psi-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi-theory)
- Primary book: Bach, *Principles of Synthetic Intelligence: PSI, an Architecture of Motivated Cognition* (OUP 2009) has chapters on modulators, motive selection and emotion; I could not read it. [record only] — [Internet Archive entry](https://archive.org/details/principlesofsynt0000bach)

**Homeostatic reinforcement learning (Keramati & Gutkin 2014, eLife)**
- State is a point H in a multidimensional homeostatic space with setpoint H*. Drive: D(H) = (Σ|h_i* - h_i|^n)^(1/m), with n > m > 1 required for the reported behavioural effects. Reward of an outcome K is **drive reduction**: r = D(H_t) - D(H_t + K) (plus an optional drive-independent term for hyperpalatable stimuli). The agent uses a sensory estimate of the outcome, not the true physiological effect. [full text, first 100k of 162k characters] — [Keramati & Gutkin 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)
- With discounting (gamma < 1), maximising discounted reward is equivalent to minimising discounted deviations from the setpoint, favouring the shortest route back to homeostasis. The framework reproduces: reward growing with deprivation and dose, **risk aversion** (the concave reward prefers frequent small deviations over rare large ones), **anticipatory/pre-emptive responding** (acting before the deficit appears when a cue predicts it), avoidance of extreme states without ever experiencing them, and the rise-then-fall of self-administration. [full text] — [Keramati & Gutkin 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)
- Action choice is by model-free RL: a reward prediction error updates the subjective value of the chosen response; the quoted sections do not specify the action-selection rule (e.g. softmax). [full text] — [Keramati & Gutkin 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)

**Drive-reduction and action-selection tests (Hull; Lorenz; Maes; Rosenblatt-Payton; Tyrrell)**
- Tyrrell (1993 thesis, Edinburgh) built a simulated environment to compare action-selection mechanisms, testing drive theory (Hull 1943), Lorenz's psychohydraulic model, Maes's spreading-activation network and Rosenblatt & Payton's hierarchical network; his own extension (ERP, a "free-flow hierarchy") is recommended as best/near optimal. In a free-flow hierarchy no decision is made until the leaf nodes, which allows **compromise candidate** behaviours that serve several drives at once. [full text of Bryson 2000 summary] — [Bryson, "Hierarchy and sequence vs. full parallelism in action selection"](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e13e4b93175437bccfc4545/t/5eaf757fabd97f4ca01d1e5d/1588557183374/hierarchy-and-sequence-vs-full-parallelism-in-action-selection.pdf); thesis record: [Tyrrell 1993, Edinburgh Research Archive](https://era.ed.ac.uk/handle/1842/20257)
- A 1994 Tyrrell paper reports deficiencies in Maes's mechanism for animal-like action-selection problems. [abstract/snippet] — [Edinburgh Research Archive](https://era.ed.ac.uk/handle/1842/20257)

**Intrinsic motivation in RL**
- Aubret, Matignon & Hassas survey: intrinsic motivation (IM) addresses exploration with sparse rewards and action/skill abstraction; their later taxonomy is information-theoretic (surprise, novelty, skill-learning); empowerment is treated as one form of IM. [abstract/snippet] — [arXiv 1908.06976](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06976v2)

**BDI**
- Evidence on BDI in games is thin and mostly negative on believability as a goal: a 2012 AIIDE paper on sales-dialogue NPCs found BDI gave representative responses and player freedom but left dialogue-quality challenges; Norling (2009) is cited as arguing believability is often a minor evaluation criterion for serious-game agents. [abstract/snippet] — [Glengarry Glen Ross: Using BDI for Sales Game Dialogues, AIIDE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12517)
- OzCHI 2024 "Mental State Modelling" reports a player study where an NPC architecture with explicit mental states scored higher overall believability than a finite-state-machine baseline (not clearly LLM-based). [abstract/snippet] — [OzCHI 2024 paper PDF](https://outbox.eait.uq.edu.au/uqsville/OzCHI2024/Long%20Papers/Mental%20State%20Modelling%20-%20A%20Philosophy%20of%20Mind%20Approach%20to%20Emergent%20Believable%20Behaviour%20in%20Non%20Player%20Characters.pdf)
- A 2025 ultimatum-game study checked LLM-generated BDI reasoning and found human annotators and a GPT-4o judge disagreed, with the judge rewarding the final action more than the reasoning chain. [abstract/snippet] — [arXiv 2505.24255](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24255)
- I did not retrieve Rao & Georgeff's BDI papers; the search found no 2024 direct evaluation of LLM-driven BDI NPCs for believability in commercial games. [search result summary]

**Appraisal and emotion models (OCC, EMA, FAtiMA)**
- FAtiMA (INESC-ID) dates from 2005, built for autonomous characters in a bullying serious game; the FAtiMA Toolkit implements OCC-based appraisal (e.g. an undesirable event maps to distress via rules) plus an explicit dialogue structure, with Unity integration. EMA follows Smith & Lazarus appraisal theory and builds appraisal/coping on the causal representations used for decision-theoretic planning; Gratch & Marsella evaluated EMA against human data to find where the model needs development. [abstract/snippet] — [FAtiMA Toolkit, arXiv 2103.03020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.03020v1.pdf); [Evaluating the modeling and use of emotion in virtual humans (USC ICT)](https://ict.usc.edu/pubs/Evaluating%20the%20modeling%20and%20use%20of%20emotion%20in%20virtual%20humans.pdf)
- A 2022 usability study of FAtiMA Toolkit found the emotion-based concepts were not as easily understood and used by the authors (content authors), a cost for design teams. [abstract/snippet] — [Towards Explainable Social Agent Authoring tools (arXiv 2206.03360)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.03360)

### Inferences
- The PSI urge idea and Keramati & Gutkin's drive function are two closely related formalisations of the same thing: need = distance from a setpoint. K&G gives a cleaner way to **combine several needs into one scalar** (the norm D) and to score any action by the **drop in D it causes**; PSI adds the practical layers (motive = need + goal, selection threshold, expectation of success, modulators for arousal).
- Because K&G's reward is concave in deficit size (n > m > 1), it already prefers dealing with many small deficits rather than letting one grow huge — a built-in guard against both starvation of minor needs and single-need fixation.
- Anticipatory behaviour (go to bed before exhausted, buy food before hunger) falls out of discounted drive-reduction if the NPC can predict deficits, which suits a daily-routine society simulation.
- Intrinsic motivation (curiosity, empowerment) is about exploration and skill acquisition; it is not needed for the eat/sleep/toilet loop and would matter only for NPCs that must learn new places or skills.
- OCC-style appraisal is best treated as a **separate, optional layer** that adds mood and relationships on top of need-driven action, not as the engine choosing among needs.

### Gaps
- Bach's book, Dörner's *Die Mechanik des Seelenwagens* and the MicroPsi 2 motivation papers were not accessible; PSI details above come from a Wikipedia summary. Exact update equations (urge growth rates, selection formula) are therefore not documented here.
- Last 62k characters of the Keramati & Gutkin article (discussion, limitations) not read; no data on scaling to many needs/agents.
- Rao & Georgeff (1995) and Jason/AgentSpeak game uses not retrieved. EMA/FAtiMA quantitative evaluation results not retrieved.
- Tyrrell's thesis text not read: only Bryson's secondary description of its conclusions is available.

## 4. Implementations in games and agent simulations, with results

### Takeaway
Games that actually ship need systems (The Sims, Dwarf Fortress) use few, cheap, per-object scored needs with personality as weights, and they reveal the failure modes (exploits, needs that are never binding). Research systems that bolt needs onto LLM agents (Humanoid Agents, AgentSociety) show needs change behaviour in the expected direction, but report weak or no direct believability measurement and rely on expensive LLM calls.

### Cited Findings
**The Sims**
- Sims decisions are driven by "motives"; in The Sims 1 these are hunger, hygiene, fun, energy, bladder, social, comfort and room; meters are described as ranging -100 to 100. Objects broadcast "advertisements" and a Sim computes a weighted score per available interaction from current motive levels, personality traits and physical proximity. Intelligence is stored in objects ("smart objects"), so adding content needs no change to the Sim AI. The Joy Booth (a deliberately high-advertisement object) is described as "SimHeroin" by Wright. In The Sims 3 the system instead keeps a map of objects that can satisfy each need. [wiki/secondary; exact curves not found] — [Draft article "Artificial intelligence in The Sims"](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/User:ElijahPepe/Artificial_intelligence_in_The_Sims) (found via search; the page itself returned 404 when fetched); [GMTK video transcript](https://amara.org/videos/cVoJS4OdmVql/en/4330642); primary paper: Forbus & Wright 2001, "Some notes on programming objects in The Sims" (named in the draft, not retrieved)
- The Sims 3 personality: each Sim has up to five traits from a pool of 80, each affecting autonomous action and emotion (Evans). [abstract/snippet] — [Evans, philarchive.org/archive/EVARPT](https://philarchive.org/archive/EVARPT); [GDC 2010 talk "Modeling Individual Personalities in The Sims 3"](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012294/Modeling-Individual-Personalities-in-The)

**Versu (Evans & Short)**
- Versu agents have goals/desires and choose, from the actions afforded by the current **social practice** (e.g. a dinner party), by utility; Evans said part of his motivation was frustration with Sims 3 characters behaving badly without clear reason. [secondary journalism] — [Modem World: Versu, making NPCs human](https://modemworld.me/2013/07/10/versu-making-npcs-human/)

**Comme il Faut / Prom Week**
- CiF is built on "social exchanges" with a responder who accepts or rejects; Prom Week has 40+ social exchanges; the designers say the model does not accurately model social cognition but takes inspiration from it for a compelling experience. No user-study numbers found. [abstract/snippet, project page] — [Prom Week AIIDE 2013 record](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12662); [Prom Week project site](https://promweek.soe.ucsc.edu/?p=295)

**Dwarf Fortress**
- Needs feed **focus**, a ratio of met to unmet need values (1:1 = neutral) that affects work speed and quality; focus is independent of overall mood/stress; unmet needs for long enough add stress and bad thoughts. Each creature's needs and their weights are shaped by personality facets, and beliefs together with facets determine how often needs must be met. [wiki/secondary, community-reported; numeric values not retrieved] — [DF wiki: Need](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Need); [DF wiki: Personality facet](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Personality_facet). Bug reports say some dwarves do not seek out ways to fulfil needs (older versions): [DF bug tracker example](https://dwarffortressbugtracker.com/view.php?id=10676)
- An academic paper on characterisation and emergent narrative in Dwarf Fortress exists (Adams) but I did not read it: [Adams PDF](https://wileywiggins.com//documents/dorf/Adams%20-%20Characterization%20and%20Emergent%20Narrative%20in%20Dwarf%20F.pdf)

**Humanoid Agents (Wang, Chiu & Chiu, EMNLP 2023 demo)**
- Extends Generative Agents with three "System 1" elements: five basic needs (fullness, fun, health, social, energy; 0-10; start at 5, energy at 10), Ekman emotion (7 states), and pairwise closeness (0-30; start 5; +1/-1 after an enjoyed/unenjoyed conversation). [full text] — [Humanoid Agents paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-demo.15.pdf)
- Update rules: a need at 3 or below triggers an LLM call to revise the plan (modifier words: 3 slightly, 2 none, 1 very, 0 extremely, with adjectives hungry/bored/unwell/tired/lonely); an activity that satisfies a need raises it by 1; otherwise each need has "a set likelihood" of dropping by 1. Decline in the simulation: fullness and health about -1 per 5 hours, with social, fun and energy declining faster (text garbled in extraction; the paper says rates are set from how fast agents satisfy needs). All agents use the same decline rates, which the authors list as a limitation. 15-minute steps, 6:00-24:00, two simulated weekdays. [full text] — [Humanoid Agents paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-demo.15.pdf)
- Results: three annotators labelled 144 activities from one day; Fleiss kappa >= 0.556 for all aspects; micro-F1 >= 0.84 for whether an activity raises fullness and energy, for emotion, and for closeness change; weaker for social, fun and health (kappa/F1 from table: fullness .972/.972, social .833/.743, fun .806/.66, health .917/.694, energy .88/.861; table read from PDF layout). Starting one need at zero changed time spent satisfying it by +156% (health), +56% (energy), +35% (fullness) but only +12% for social, because agents already spent most of the day on fun (11 of 18 h) and social activity (8.75 h) so the effect "dissipates early in the day". [full text] — [Humanoid Agents paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-demo.15.pdf)
- No direct believability rating by external judges is reported (evaluation is need-prediction accuracy and behaviour sensitivity). [full text] — same source

**Generative Agents (Park et al. 2023) and the believability benchmark for LLM agents**
- 25 agents; 100 Prolific evaluators ranked five conditions; TrueSkill mu: full architecture 29.89 (sigma 0.72), observation+planning without reflection 26.88, observation only 25.64, human crowdworker role-play 22.95, no memory components 21.21; Kruskal-Wallis H(4)=150.29, p<0.001. Costs: the 2-day, 25-agent run cost "thousands of dollars" in tokens; agents sometimes retrieved memories poorly, embellished, and behaved overly formal and cooperative. There is no explicit need or drive state in this architecture. [full text] — [Generative Agents (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)

**AgentSociety**
- Engine reported to support up to 10k agents averaging 500 interactions per day (abstract: over 10k agents, 5 million interactions); emotion as six emotions rated 0-10; cognition as attitude scores 0-10; needs updated from behaviour, events and psychological state. Cost figures and needs-specific validation were not in the part I could read. [full text, first half] — [AgentSociety arXiv 2502.08691](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08691v1)

### Inferences
- Both industry games and the research platform use needs the same way: a small vector of scalars with simple decay and discrete satisfaction events, with the action chosen by scoring candidate activities/objects against it. This is already cheap enough for thousands of NPCs with no LLM call per decision; Humanoid Agents' and AgentSociety's LLM-per-decision design is what makes them costly, not the need model.
- Humanoid Agents' result that "social" and "fun" barely changed behaviour when set to zero is a design warning: a need only shapes behaviour if the schedule leaves it **binding** (time or resource scarcity). In a Gothic society, work, curfew and distance should compete for the same hours so needs actually trade off.
- The Joy Booth shows what happens when a satisfier's advertised value is unbounded: clamp advertised value to the real deficit.
- The Sims 3 change (need -> list of satisfying objects) suggests the efficient implementation: precompute, per need, an index of satisfier locations/items/NPCs, and query it at decision time.
- Evidence of *measured believability gain* from need models specifically is missing: Humanoid Agents measured prediction agreement, not observer ratings; Generative Agents measured believability but without a needs ablation.

### Gaps
- Exact Sims motive decay constants and response curves; Dwarf Fortress numeric need thresholds; Versu and CiF evaluation data; FAtiMA and EMA believability studies with numbers.
- No retrieved study that runs a controlled A/B of "with needs" versus "without needs" and scores observer believability.
- AgentSociety validation numbers and compute cost (second half of the paper unread).
- Other LLM-agent need/BDI papers from 2024-2026 (beyond those named above) were not surveyed systematically.

## 5. Practical guidance: how many needs, curves, combining urgency, personality modulation, avoiding oscillation and starvation

### Takeaway
The sources support a layered design: a small vector of homeostatic needs, a smooth urgency function that grows convexly with deficit, an action choice that scores candidate satisfiers by how much total urgency they remove (allowing multi-need "compromise" actions), and an explicit persistence mechanism. Most numbers are not established in the literature and must be tuned and tested in your own simulation.

### Cited Findings
- **How many needs exist in published systems:** Humanoid Agents 5 ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-demo.15.pdf)); The Sims 1: 8 motives ([draft article](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/User:ElijahPepe/Artificial_intelligence_in_The_Sims), secondary); Kenrick 2010: 7 motive systems ([paper](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/KenrickGriskeviciusNeubergSchaller2010.pdf)); Tay & Diener: 6 need classes ([paper](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)); Max-Neef: 9 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred_Max-Neef%27s_Fundamental_human_needs)); Reiss: 16 ([OSU](https://news.osu.edu/new-theory-of-motivation-lists-16-basic-desires-that-guide-us/)); PSI: three families (physiological, social, cognitive) with a handful of urges ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi-theory)).
- **Combining needs, mathematically:** Keramati & Gutkin define overall drive as the norm of all deficits, D = (Σ|h* - h|^n)^(1/m), with n > m > 1, and score outcomes by the change in D ([eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)). The Sims scores each interaction by a weighted function of motive levels, personality and distance ([draft article](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/User:ElijahPepe/Artificial_intelligence_in_The_Sims), secondary). Dwarf Fortress uses a met/unmet ratio (focus) ([DF wiki](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Need)). Mark & Dill's utility-AI lecture slides normalise each input with a **response curve**, combine, and then select the highest score, a weighted random from all options, or a weighted random from the top n ([slides](https://www.sambuz.com/doc/improving-improving-ai-decision-modeling-ai-decision-ppt-presentation-788939)).
- **Winner-take-all versus compromise:** Tyrrell's simulation found a free-flow hierarchy (decide only at leaf actions, so one action can serve several drives) best among five mechanisms; Bryson's later POSH (hierarchy with limited reactivity and persistence) beat it in 3 of 4 worlds: mean mating score in the standard world 9.12 (SE 0.19) versus 8.09 (SE 0.17); also better in two variant worlds (9.67 vs 8.16; 4.02 vs 3.61) but worse in one (11.23 vs 13.38) ([Bryson](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e13e4b93175437bccfc4545/t/5eaf757fabd97f4ca01d1e5d/1588557183374/hierarchy-and-sequence-vs-full-parallelism-in-action-selection.pdf), table read from PDF layout). Bryson also cites Humphrys evaluating final-selection strategies and favouring "maximizing the least unhappiness" of the competing units, and notes that too much priority to perceived predators "disrupts behavior coherence" while too little reduces lifespan; she tuned six thresholds by random search over 4,400 trials, improving mean performance 25% ([same paper](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e13e4b93175437bccfc4545/t/5eaf757fabd97f4ca01d1e5d/1588557183374/hierarchy-and-sequence-vs-full-parallelism-in-action-selection.pdf)).
- **Oscillation / persistence:** Dave Mark's "decision momentum" idea (making each decision include all relevant information to stop AIs flipping targets or shuffling back and forth) is described in a book review ([GBGames review of Behavioral Mathematics for Game AI](https://www.gbgames.com/2017/02/13/book-review-behavioral-mathematics-for-game-ai-by-dave-mark/), secondary). PSI uses a **selection threshold** modulator to control how stable the dominant motive is against competing motives ([Wikipedia: Psi-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi-theory)). POSH gives persistence through hierarchy ([Bryson](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e13e4b93175437bccfc4545/t/5eaf757fabd97f4ca01d1e5d/1588557183374/hierarchy-and-sequence-vs-full-parallelism-in-action-selection.pdf)).
- **Starvation of low priority:** K&G's concave reward favours "frequent small deviations from the setpoint ... over rare drastic deviations" ([eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)); Kenrick's overlapping (not replacing) motives mean low-level needs keep competing ([paper](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/KenrickGriskeviciusNeubergSchaller2010.pdf)); Tay & Diener found needs contribute independently and additively ([paper](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)).
- **Personality modulating needs:** Dwarf Fortress lets facets and beliefs set which needs a dwarf has, their weights and how often they must be met ([DF wiki](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Need)); Sims 3 traits affect autonomous action and emotion ([Evans](https://philarchive.org/archive/EVARPT)); Humanoid Agents' authors name per-character decline rates (e.g. extroverts' faster social decline) as the planned extension ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-demo.15.pdf)); PSI makes modulator ranges (and hence emotional/behavioural style) individual ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psi-theory)).
- **Time-scales used:** Humanoid Agents: 15-minute steps, needs 0-10, replan trigger at <=3 ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-demo.15.pdf)); The Sims: -100..100 meters ([draft article](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/User:ElijahPepe/Artificial_intelligence_in_The_Sims), secondary).

### Inferences
Candidate design (not validated by the sources; all numbers are starting points to tune):
1. **State per NPC:** a short vector of 6-10 homeostatic needs (for example food, thirst, sleep/energy, toilet, rest/comfort, safety, social, status/work-achievement, fun/ritual), each normalised 0..1 deficit, plus per-NPC decay-rate multipliers (personality, age, job) and a few slow variables (SDT-like competence/autonomy/relatedness, or Reiss/Schwartz-like value weights). More than about 10 raises tuning cost with little evidence of added believability; unbounded lists (Reiss 16, Max-Neef 36 cells) fit better as *tags on satisfiers* than as separate decaying meters.
2. **Urgency:** u_i = w_i * deficit_i^p with p about 2 (convex, in the spirit of n > m > 1), and w_i raised by context cues (Kenrick: a threat nearby boosts safety, food smell boosts hunger). Strict "survival override" only for hard thresholds (starving, exhausted, attacked), implemented as a steep rise rather than a gate, so higher needs are never fully switched off.
3. **Action choice:** for each affordance (object, place, person, task) compute gain = D(before) - D(after predicted) minus travel/time cost, as in drive-reduction RL; this automatically values multi-need actions (eating at an inn also socialises) and anticipates (go to bed before exhaustion) if the NPC predicts the deficit a few hours ahead. Pick softmax or weighted-random from the top n rather than strict argmax to avoid identical behaviour, and to match the utility-AI literature.
4. **Persistence:** add a commitment term (current activity gets a bonus that decays with time and is released when its need drops under a satisfaction target, PSI's selection threshold) plus a minimum dwell time and a cool-down per just-satisfied need; interruption only if another urgency exceeds current by a margin. This addresses oscillation (Mark's momentum, PSI's selection threshold, POSH persistence), which the sources describe but give no numbers for.
5. **Starvation guard:** because urgency is convex and summed (or norm-combined), a low-priority need grows until it wins; add a hard cap (a need beyond a "critical" band forces an interrupt) and ensure the world schedule leaves slots in which satisfiers are reachable (Humanoid Agents: needs that are never binding do not shape behaviour).
6. **Personality:** map a small trait set (for example 5-6 dimensions including Honesty-Humility) to decay rates, weights w_i, risk/commitment thresholds and which satisfiers an NPC accepts (Max-Neef's needs versus satisfiers). Expect effect sizes around r .2-.3, so keep large per-NPC and per-decision variation (Fleeson).
7. **Scale:** with N needs and A candidate affordances, a decision is O(N x A) arithmetic with precomputed need-to-satisfier indexes; thousands of NPCs are feasible without any network inference, and a learned model (the project's "specialised efficient model") can be trained to imitate or tune this scoring, with the hand-written utility function as the baseline and test oracle.
8. **Validation:** Humanoid Agents' behaviour-sensitivity test (zero one need at start, measure change in time spent satisfying it) is a cheap regression test for whether each need actually shapes the schedule; Tyrrell/Bryson's approach (random threshold sweeps over thousands of runs, compare means with a significance test) is the precedent for tuning thresholds.

### Gaps
- No source gives validated decay constants, curve exponents or commitment/hysteresis parameters for human-like daily-need behaviour; all numbers above are design choices to be tuned.
- I did not find a game-AI source (Game AI Pro chapter or GDC talk text) that documents the Sims' or Dwarf Fortress's motive curve formulas, nor a published comparison of max / weighted-sum / priority-layer combination rules on believability. The Atkinson & Birch "dynamics of action" model (a classical psychological model of action selection with inertia) was not searched.
- No retrieved evidence on how a learned (neural) policy distilled from a utility-based need controller performs versus the controller itself for NPC societies.
