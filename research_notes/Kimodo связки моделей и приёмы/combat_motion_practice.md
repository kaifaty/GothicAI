# Combat, weapon and acrobatic animation from generative motion models: practitioner techniques and game-studio ML practice (state as of 2026-11-01)

Context for the report writer: the project generates base motion with NVIDIA Kimodo (SOMA skeleton, text + keyframe constraints), retargets to the Gothic 2 Biped skeleton (BIP01, 34 nodes, 25 fps) and finishes in Blender. On the 2026-11-01 probe dashes, rolls and a falling victim worked; the overhead strike had no downswing, the riposte prompt made the attacker double over, the barge had no shoulder contact, the wrist flailed because Kimodo knows no sword, and dashes were slower than the target (active phase 0.45–0.55 s for 1.5–3 m).

Research budget note: about 26 tool calls. Several studio-specific questions (Remedy, Guerrilla, CD Projekt, Activision, No Rest for the Wicked animators) returned no primary sources; they are listed under Gaps rather than filled from memory.

## 1. Getting weapon actions out of text/constraint motion models (Kimodo and comparable)

### Takeaway
Kimodo has no notion of props or objects (the authors call object interaction future work), so a sword strike cannot come from text alone: the reliable route is to drive the attack with sparse full-body and right-hand position+rotation keyframes taken from existing game attacks (fewer than 20 per constraint type, post-processing on so they are hit exactly), keep prompts about the attacker's own body in "A person…" form with one or two behaviours, and then replace the sword arm's wrist/forearm with an authored blade path in Blender. No commercially licensed text-to-motion model trained on weapon or sword data was found.

### Cited Findings
**What Kimodo is trained on and can be told (primary: NVIDIA paper, docs, repo)**
- Kimodo is trained on Bones Rigplay, a studio optical mocap set of about 700 hours from 170 subjects; categories include locomotion, gestures, everyday activities, common object interactions, "videogame combat", dancing and athletics; the paper does not mention weapons, swords or martial arts specifically — [Kimodo paper, arXiv 2603.15546](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15546v1)
- Scene and object interaction is named as a future challenge ("Gathering data for this problem becomes even more challenging"); Kimodo is "designed as an offline motion authoring model", not a runtime controller — [Kimodo paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15546v1)
- Constraint types: full-body keyframes, end-effector (hand/foot) position and rotation keyframes, 2D root waypoints, dense 2D root paths and foot-contact patterns — [Kimodo paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15546v1)
- How constraints are enforced: imputation (target pose features overwrite the noisy motion through a binary mask), a second training phase with sampled constraint patterns so the model learns to follow them, and classifier-free guidance with separate text and constraint weights (default 2 each); gradient guidance was not used because it gave minimal gain and was unstable — [Kimodo paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15546v1)
- API classes: `FullBodyConstraintSet(skeleton, frame_indices, global_joints_positions, global_joints_rots, …)`, `EndEffectorConstraintSet(…, joint_names=…)` with ready subclasses `LeftHandConstraintSet`/`RightHandConstraintSet` (joint `RightHand`) and foot sets, and `Root2DConstraintSet(skeleton, frame_indices, smooth_root_2d, global_root_heading=None)`; end-effector inputs are global positions and global rotations — [Kimodo API: constraints](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/kimodo/docs/api_reference/constraints.html)
- A secondary summary reports end-effector accuracy of 3.09 cm position and 4.18° rotation average error (not verified against the paper) — [Learn AI wiki: Kimodo](https://ai.miraheze.org/wiki/Kimodo)
- Best practices from the official limitations page: begin each prompt with "A person…"; keep each prompt to one or at most two behaviours; use a medium level of detail; "Prompts for actions outside of these categories will likely give bad results"; with multiple prompts each must carry enough information on its own, and "the second prompt must devote some of its duration to performing a smooth transition"; maximum 10 s per prompt — [Kimodo docs: limitations](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/kimodo/docs/key_concepts/limitations.html)
- Constraint best practices: performance is best with fewer than 20 constrained keyframes per constraint type (dense 2D root paths excepted; the model is mainly trained on sparse temporal constraints); avoid constraints that contradict the text or each other ("can lead to artifacts or constraints that are ignored"); trade constraint vs text accuracy by adjusting the classifier-free guidance weights; enable post-processing when foot contact and hitting constraints matter, because "The model by itself can generate foot skating and will not exactly hit constraints" — [Kimodo docs: limitations](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/kimodo/docs/key_concepts/limitations.html)
- Post-processing (foot-skate and constraint cleanup) is on by default; `--no-postprocess` disables it; constraints are passed with `--constraints` as a file (for example one saved from the web demo); the CLI takes a single text or a sequence of texts, and the 2026-04-24 changelog notes "improved multi-prompt generation" with transition parameters in the Python API — [Kimodo GitHub](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/kimodo)
- `kimodo.postprocess.post_process_motion()` takes local rotations, root positions, foot-contact labels, the skeleton and optionally the constraint list (`contact_threshold=0.5`, `root_margin=0.04`) and returns corrected rotations, root and global joints; the paper says a short optimization makes motions hit constraints exactly and is used in the demo and codebase — [Kimodo API: post-processing](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/kimodo/docs/api_reference/post-processing.html); [Kimodo paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15546v1)
- The best SOMA model runs at 30 fps; output NPZ contains joint positions, rotation matrices, foot contacts and root data — [Kimodo paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15546v1); [Kimodo GitHub](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/kimodo)

**Licences relevant to this route**
- Kimodo code is Apache-2.0; SOMA checkpoints (RP-v1, RP-v1.1, RP-v1.2, RP-ArdyRep-v1.2, SEED-v1, SEED-v1.1) and G1 checkpoints are under the NVIDIA Open Model License; the SMPL-X RP-v1 checkpoint is under NVIDIA's internal scientific R&D licence (not usable for a commercial game). The RP models are trained on proprietary Bones Rigplay 1 (~700 h); SEED models on BONES-SEED (~288 h of publicly available mocap) and are less capable — [Kimodo GitHub](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/kimodo); [HF: Kimodo-SOMA-RP-v1](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Kimodo-SOMA-RP-v1); [HF: Kimodo-SMPLX-RP-v1](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Kimodo-SMPLX-RP-v1)
- The docs describe the training data as "commercially-friendly optical motion capture" — [Kimodo docs](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/kimodo/docs)
- NVIDIA Open Model License (version of 2025-10-24): "Models are commercially usable"; worldwide royalty-free grant including the right to sell; "NVIDIA claims no ownership rights in outputs"; derivative models are allowed and owned by the creator; if the model itself is distributed, ship the agreement and the notice "Licensed by NVIDIA Corporation under the NVIDIA Open Model License"; rights terminate automatically if a safety guardrail is circumvented without a substitute, or on filing IP litigation against the model; no EU-specific restriction, export/sanctions compliance required — [NVIDIA Open Model License](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/agreements/enterprise-software/nvidia-open-model-license/)

**Weapon/object-aware generators found**
- Hand–object text-to-motion work such as Text2HOI (CVPR 2024; datasets H2O, GRAB, ARCTIC) covers general objects, not weapons or fencing; HOI-Diff (text-driven human–object interaction diffusion) is in the same family. No sword/fencing text-to-motion model was found — [Text2HOI](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00562v2); [Text2HOI GitHub](https://github.com/JunukCha/Text2HOI); [HOI-Diff](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.06553)
- EA's "Neural Animation Layering for Synthesizing Martial Arts Movements" (Starke, Zhao, Zinno, Komura; SIGGRAPH 2021) imitates animation layering with networks: a motion generator maps edited trajectories to full-body motion while separately trained control modules supply task inputs; it produces punching, kicking, avoiding, fighter signature moves and clinching, and lets animators modify or combine movements after training — [SIGGRAPH history entry](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=121207); [paper PDF](https://www.ipab.inf.ed.ac.uk/cgvu/starke2021.pdf)
- Tencent's MoreFun (GDC 2026) showed an AI in-betweening tool on existing kung-fu animations: with their earlier "math-based" procedural in-betweens a sword clipped through a swordfighter's straw hat, with the AI version the blade swung around it; training data came from hired kung-fu practitioners captured with AI tracking on a small stage — [Game Developer, 2026-03-24](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/tencent-s-genai-animation-tools-don-t-seem-to-make-games-more-fun)

### Inferences
- **Keyframe the strike, don't describe it.** Because objects are out of distribution and only "videogame combat" in general is in the data, the reliable handle on a sword strike is the end-effector constraint on `RightHand` (position and rotation together) at 3–5 beats sampled from the original Gothic attack clips: guard, wind-up apex, contact, end of follow-through, back to guard. That stays well under the 20-keyframe limit. Map the original BIP01 R Hand transform into SOMA space with the same retarget used in reverse.
- **Overhead strike with a hold (no downswing on the probe):** the model followed "raise the sword" and had no reason to come down. Put a full-body keyframe on the impact pose (taken from Gothic's own 2H overhead) at the impact frame, and a right-hand (and left-hand, if two-handed) keyframe on the apex for 2–3 consecutive frames to create the hold. Alternatively use the timeline with two prompts ("A person raises both arms high above the head and holds still" → "A person swings both arms down hard in front of the body"), remembering the second prompt spends part of its duration on the transition. Raise the constraint CFG weight above the default 2 if the keyframes are ignored, and keep post-processing on so they are hit exactly.
- **Riposte (attacker doubled over):** the prompt "thrusts a sword into the stomach of an enemy" contains a body part plus a weapon, and in mocap captions that pattern most often labels the person being stabbed. Describe only the attacker's own body and direction, at medium detail, starting with "A person…": e.g. "A person lunges forward and thrusts the right arm straight ahead at chest height, then pulls it back." Put the target point in a right-hand position keyframe instead of in words. The doubled-over result is itself a usable victim clip (see section 2).
- **Shoulder barge (no contact):** combine a `Root2DConstraintSet` path that accelerates and then stops at the contact frame with a full-body keyframe of the contact pose (lead shoulder forward, torso leaning into it). Contact with the victim is then made by alignment, not by generation.
- **Partial-body generation:** Kimodo exposes no body-part mask beyond the end-effector sets, so the practical "legs and root from the model, sword arm from keys" split is done after generation: keep root, legs and spine from Kimodo and layer the sword arm from the original clip or from hand keys (layered/override blend in Blender). EA's neural animation layering shows the layering paradigm is the standard way to combine a generator with task-specific control.
- **Licence:** use only SOMA RP checkpoints (NVIDIA Open Model License); outputs are owned by the user and the game ships only animation, so the notice obligation applies only if the model is redistributed. Do not use the SMPL-X checkpoint.

## 2. Paired / two-person interaction generation (attacker + victim) and how studios author paired takedowns

### Takeaway
All the well-known interaction generators (InterGen, InterControl, in2IN and their 2025–2026 follow-ups) are built on research-only data (InterHuman is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, Inter-X research-only), so none is usable in a commercial EU game; the commercially safe route is the studio one: generate or author attacker and victim separately, align them on a shared root offset and sync frames (Assassin's Creed-style paired animation, Gears of War 4 warp points, Unreal Contextual Animation + Motion Warping), and fix contacts with interaction-mesh-style constraints.

### Cited Findings
**Generators and their licences**
- A 2025 survey lists ReMoS (ECCV 2024), in2IN (CVPRW 2024), InterControl (NeurIPS 2024) and InterGen (IJCV 2024) as the main two-person methods — [Survey on Human Interaction Motion Generation](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.12763v1); curated list: [Awesome-Human-Interaction-Motion-Generation](https://github.com/soraproducer/awesome-human-interaction-motion-generation)
- InterHuman (InterGen's dataset) is released under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, for non-commercial purposes, with redistribution prohibited — [InterGen GitHub](https://github.com/tr3e/InterGen)
- Inter-X is listed as "Research use only (non-commercial)" and additionally requires the SMPL-X licence (secondary catalogue; official repo not checked) — [Inter-X listing](https://leooo-huang.github.io/awesome-human-activity-recognition/datasets/emerging/interx/)
- ReMoS's ReMoCap dataset: ~275.7K frames of two-person mocap with multiview video and finger articulation, covering Lindy Hop and the martial art Ninjutsu; the authors stated they would release code and data; no licence text was found — [ReMoS arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17057v3); [ReMoS project page](https://vcai.mpi-inf.mpg.de/projects/remos/)
- Newer work keeps the same backbones: "Multi-Person Interaction Generation from Two-Person Motion Priors" (SIGGRAPH 2025) uses InterGen and in2IN as backbones; PhysiGen (ICASSP 2026) adds collision-aware physical constraints on InterGen and in2IN; Interact2Ar (arXiv 2512.19692, Dec 2025) is an autoregressive diffusion model for full-body human–human interaction — [SIGGRAPH 2025 paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3721238.3730688); [PhysiGen readme](https://github.com/iSEE-Laboratory/PhysiGen/blob/main/readme.md); [Interact2Ar](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.19692)

**Contact-preserving adaptation of close interactions (methods, not datasets)**
- Ho, Komura & Tai (ACM TOG 2010) "interaction mesh": a structure that represents spatial relationships between two characters; edits minimise its local deformation, which keeps relationships and reduces interpenetration; demonstrated on dancing, wrestling and sword fighting, with or without contact — [Northumbria research portal](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/spatial-relationship-preserving-character-motion-adaptation/)
- Zhang, Gopinath, Ye, Hodgins, Turk & Won (SIGGRAPH 2023) use an interaction graph of distances between landmark pairs as an RL reward, to "clean up" mocap into physically plausible interactions or to retarget interactions to characters of different sizes while keeping the contacts — [arXiv 2305.20041](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.20041)
- MeshRet (arXiv 2410.20986, 2024) extends this with dense mesh interaction fields for geometry-aware retargeting — [MeshRet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.20986v2)

**How games author paired moves**
- Assassin's Creed-style paired animation: attacker and victim align and play one shared animation, neither moving independently, so the animator can manipulate the victim's body directly; it suits moves where one input finishes the enemy, and does not extend to hitting several enemies without custom transitions — [critpoints: What is paired animation](https://critpoints.net/2020/03/24/what-the-fuck-is-paired-animation/)
- Gears of War 4 generalised ad-hoc spatial fitting by introducing "warp points" for motion warping — [GDC Vault: Motion Warping in Gears of War 4](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024219/Motion-Warping-in-Gears-of)
- EA's GDC Animation Summit session covers environmental and synchronised multi-character motion-matched interactions in Madden and FIFA — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027465/Animation-Summit-Environmental-and-Motion)
- In Unreal Engine 5 finishers/takedowns are typically built with the Contextual Animation and Motion Warping plugins (community tutorial) — [YouTube tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A3y4YT9YQI8)

### Inferences
- **Riposte pipeline without a paired model:** (1) generate the attacker's thrust alone with a right-hand keyframe at the victim's torso point on the contact frame; (2) generate the victim alone ("A person is hit in the stomach and doubles over, then falls" — exactly the motion Kimodo already produced by accident, and falling victims worked on the probe); (3) author both in one shared space: a fixed root offset/heading between attacker and victim at the sync frame, the victim's reaction starting on the attacker's contact frame; (4) in the engine, warp both roots to that offset before the clip starts (the warp-point idea). Gothic has no contextual-animation system, so the alignment is a small engine step.
- **Barge:** same pattern — the attacker clip owns the contact frame and contact pose; the victim's stagger/knockdown starts at that frame; shoulder–chest contact is kept by aligning roots plus a short IK/interaction-mesh pass in Blender on the frames around contact.
- Treat any model trained on InterHuman, Inter-X or unlicensed ReMoCap as excluded, consistent with the project's existing exclusion of AMASS-trained non-commercial models; this includes InterControl and in2IN, which to my knowledge also train/evaluate on InterHuman (not re-verified here).

### Gaps
- Licence files of InterControl, in2IN, ReMoS code repos, PhysiGen and Interact2Ar were not read; only the dataset licences above were confirmed.
- No GDC talk with concrete sync-point/root-offset math for melee paired kills (e.g. The Last of Us Part II, God of War, Ghost of Tsushima) was retrieved.
- No commercially licensed two-person combat mocap dataset was found.

## 3. Game feel: retiming generated motion, time-warping with foot contacts, dodge/roll design

### Takeaway
Combat game feel comes from very short or near-zero anticipation on defensive moves, a clear active phase and back-loaded recovery (Dark Souls/Elden Ring roll: about 13 invulnerable frames and 8 recovery frames at 30 fps, i.e. ~0.43 s + ~0.27 s), while attacks keep readable anticipation sized against responsiveness; generated motion should therefore be retimed with a monotonic, piecewise time-warp (compress anticipation, keep the impact frame, hold, then recovery), with foot-contact labels warped by the same map and foot locking re-applied, and root distance scaled separately (stride warping) to hit the 1.5–3 m targets.

### Cited Findings
- Dark Souls' roll starts almost immediately on input, with "little to no anticipation"; i-frames start almost at once and "the vulnerable frames of animation are almost entirely back-loaded on the action" — [Parry Everything: The Dark Souls Dodge Roll](https://parryeverything.com/2021/07/30/the-dark-souls-dodge-roll-immediacy-in-player-action/)
- Elden Ring frame data at 30 fps: light and medium rolls both have 13 i-frames and 8 recovery frames; heavy roll (70–99.9 % load) 12 i-frames and 16 recovery frames; light load mainly adds roll distance. Other sources give 14 i-frames or claim light rolls have more; those are treated as unreliable — [Gamertag Mythras guide](https://gamertagmythras.com/blog/elden-ring/elden-ring-equip-load-guide); [Elden Ring wiki: I-frames](https://eldenring.wiki.gg/wiki/Invincibility_Frames) (conflicting 14-frame figure); [Cyberpost](https://cyberpost.co/what-are-the-invincibility-frames-in-elden-ring/) (internally inconsistent)
- GDKeys splits every attack into anticipation, attack and recovery; too long an anticipation makes controls feel unresponsive; Hollow Knight is cited for a single anticipation frame with near-instant recovery; the attacker is extremely vulnerable during anticipation and recovery (search snippet; page returned HTTP 503 when fetched) — [GDKeys: Anatomy of an Attack](https://gdkeys.com/keys-to-combat-design-1-anatomy-of-an-attack/)
- For Honor (Ubisoft Montreal) used motion matching (GDC 2016 talk by Simon Clavet) to close the gap between input and screen; a 2018 Ubisoft update shifted link/cancel timings so players had to input 33 ms earlier, showing how tightly cancel windows are tuned — [gameanim.com on GDC 2016](https://www.gameanim.com/?p=13538); [Ubisoft For Honor input-lag post (FR)](https://www.ubisoft.com/fr-fr/game/for-honor/news-updates/4qgcoZf3m61lpWD8GlN4JO/for-honor-et-linput-lag)
- EA's GDC talk "Animation Warping for Responsiveness in FIFA Soccer" is the classic reference for warping animation to gain responsiveness — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012342/Animation-Warping-for-Responsiveness-in)
- Root motion for dodges must live on the root bone; animating only the pelvis does not drive the capsule, and a roll without root motion slides back after the montage ends — [Unreal forum thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-to-use-attack-dodge-animations-without-root-motion/679321); [UE5 dodge roll root-motion tutorial](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/community-tutorial-how-to-make-a-dodge-roll-with-root-motion-in-unreal-engine-5/1214245)
- Unreal's IK Retargeter offers Speed Planting and Stride Warping, which require IK goals on the feet — [UE docs: IK Rig retargeting](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)
- Kimodo outputs per-frame foot-contact labels, and its post-processing uses them for foot locking (`post_process_motion(…, contact_threshold=0.5)`) — [Kimodo API: post-processing](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/kimodo/docs/api_reference/post-processing.html)

### Inferences
- **Numbers for Gothic at 25 fps:** 13 i-frames at 30 fps = 0.43 s ≈ 11 frames at 25 fps; 8 recovery frames = 0.27 s ≈ 7 frames. The project's target active phase of 0.45–0.55 s is in the same band as a Souls roll. 1.5–3 m in 0.45–0.55 s is an average of 2.7–6.7 m/s.
- **Retime in the resample step.** Kimodo SOMA runs at 30 fps and Gothic at 25 fps, so a resample is already needed; make it a monotonic piecewise time-warp t' = f(t) instead of a uniform 30→25: compress the anticipation to 1–3 frames for dodges (2–5 for light attacks), keep the impact/contact frame on an integer frame, add a 1–3 frame hold on impact for heavy hits, and stretch recovery last. Warp the foot-contact labels with the same f and re-run foot locking on the warped clip (Kimodo's own `post_process_motion` or Blender IK) so contacts survive.
- **Distance separately from timing:** scale the root translation of the active phase to the design distance (stride-warping idea) and let foot IK absorb the change, instead of making the whole body faster.
- **Generate long, cut short:** Kimodo produces realistic, therefore slow, dashes. Generate with a dense 2D root path that already covers the target distance in the target time (dense root paths are the one constraint Kimodo handles densely), then trim the lead-in so the first frame is already moving.

### Gaps
- No GDC talk or interview from FromSoftware, Ubisoft (For Honor animators) or Moon Studios (No Rest for the Wicked) on how their dodge/roll animations are keyed was found; No Rest for the Wicked dodge timings were not found.
- The GDKeys page could not be fetched (HTTP 503), so only its search snippet is cited.
- No paper-level source on time-warping generated motion with contact preservation was retrieved (classic motion-warping literature was not checked).

## 4. Keeping a held weapon stable and cleaning foot contacts after retargeting

### Takeaway
Since the generator has no sword, the wrist must be overwritten, not cleaned: treat the blade as a rigid child of the hand, author the blade arc (hilt path and blade direction) for wind-up, impact and recovery, and solve the forearm/wrist with two-bone or full-body IK to that path with the lower body pinned; feet are handled by contact-label foot locking plus IK goals. Unreal's retargeter formalises exactly this (hand IK goals, Full Body IK with root pinned for two-handed weapons, dedicated Weapon Goals and Pin Bone ops).

### Cited Findings
- Unreal's IK Retargeter can keep precise hand or foot contact points with IK; for a two-handed weapon Epic's guide adds a Full Body IK solver on the target rig with goals on both hands and Root Behavior "Pin to Input" so the lower body does not move when the hand goals move; feet get IK goals to stay planted — [UE docs: IK Rig retargeting](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine); [UE docs: retargeting bipeds](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/retargeting-bipeds-with-ik-rig-in-unreal-engine)
- The UE 5.7 Python API lists separate retarget operations including a Pin Bone Op and a Weapon Goals Op — [UE Python API: IKRetargeter](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/IKRetargeter?application_version=5.7)
- A community thread (UE 5.1 era) built root→gun, gun→left hand and gun→right hand chains to retarget a weapon and found precision a problem (anecdotal) — [Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/ik-retargeter-is-not-retargeting-ik-bone-information/525060?page=3)
- Kimodo's own foot-skate cleanup uses predicted contacts with foot locking and IK and is on by default — [Kimodo paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15546v1); [Kimodo GitHub](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/kimodo)
- Tencent MoreFun's GDC 2026 AI in-betweening was aimed at less clipping and sliding of a blade around the body (the sword passed around a straw hat instead of through it) — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/tencent-s-genai-animation-tools-don-t-seem-to-make-games-more-fun)

### Inferences
- **Blade-first authoring in Blender:** add a "sword" empty parented to the hand bone at the grip (in Gothic the weapon hangs on the right-hand slot node; verify the node name in OpenGothic), animate the blade's hilt position and blade direction as a designed arc with keys at guard, wind-up apex, impact and follow-through, then constrain the hand back to it (Child Of / Damped Track) and solve the arm with two-bone IK and a pole target at the elbow. The model's wrist rotation is discarded; only shoulder/torso motion is kept from the generator.
- **Hand rotation keyframes in Kimodo first:** feeding `RightHandConstraintSet` with both position and rotation at 3–5 beats (section 1) reduces the flailing before Blender, so the IK override has less to fix.
- **Penetration check:** after IK, test the blade segment against simple capsules for the body and the ground plane per frame and flag frames where it passes through; fix those by moving the arc, not the wrist.
- **Pin the lower body while editing the arm** (the "Pin to Input" idea) so arm IK does not drag the pelvis and break foot contacts; re-run foot locking at the end.

### Gaps
- No studio talk on blade-arc authoring or weapon-trail-driven IK was retrieved.
- Blender-specific add-ons (e.g. Auto-Rig Pro remap, Rokoko retargeting) were not researched; their licences are unchecked.

## 5. Retargeting generated motion to a game skeleton with different proportions (Biped), root motion, contacts

### Takeaway
Practice in engines is chain-based retargeting with a defined retarget root (usually the pelvis) so root motion is transferred proportionally, plus IK goals on hands and feet to restore contacts after proportion changes; for close contacts between characters the research answer is interaction-mesh / interaction-graph constraints. For the game, root motion must be on the root node that the engine reads, not only on the pelvis.

### Cited Findings
- The IK Retargeter maps animation between rigs with different bone counts, names and orientations; each IK Rig must define a retarget root (typically pelvis/hips) "in order for the character's root motion to be defined and transferred in a proportional way"; arm chains typically end at the hand — [UE docs: IK Rig retargeting](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)
- In UE 5.7 the pelvis op settings replace the deprecated root settings; the export root-lock setting affects exported animation only — [UE Python API: IKRetargeter](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/IKRetargeter?application_version=5.7)
- Clips with motion kept on the hips need the root and hip bones fixed in Blender/Maya before root motion works — [Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-to-make-dodge-roll-in-ue5-and-use-root-motion/2754157)
- Interaction-preserving retargeting across body sizes: interaction mesh (Ho et al. 2010), interaction graph (Zhang et al. 2023), MeshRet (2024) — [Northumbria](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/spatial-relationship-preserving-character-motion-adaptation/); [arXiv 2305.20041](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.20041); [MeshRet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.20986v2)
- Kimodo's official retarget paths are SOMA→G1 and SMPL-X→robots via GMR; no BVH export or game-skeleton retargeter is provided — [Kimodo GitHub](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/kimodo)

### Inferences
- For SOMA→BIP01, retarget rotations per chain, scale root translation by the leg-length ratio (proportional root motion), then re-solve feet with IK to the contact labels and hands with IK to the weapon path; extract root motion onto the node the Gothic engine reads for movement (verify in OpenGothic which node carries translation in `.MAN` clips).
- For paired moves, retarget each character first, then restore the attacker–victim contact points on the aligned pair (interaction-mesh idea applied as a few IK targets on contact frames), because proportion changes move the contact points.

### Gaps
- No public SOMA→3ds Max Biped retargeting tool or write-up was found.

## 6. Game-industry practice with ML for combat and acrobatic animation

### Takeaway
Shipped studio ML in animation is mostly selection and deformation, not generation: Ubisoft's learned motion matching (used in Far Cry 6 via Choreograph) and For Honor's motion matching, EA's neural layering research on martial arts, EA SEED's RL goalkeeper and face tools, and in 2026 Tencent's AI in-betweening of kung-fu sword strikes; animators still own the authored poses, timing and capture direction, and the press reception of generative combat animation at GDC 2026 was sceptical.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The industry pattern matches the project's plan: ML provides bulk motion or selection, while hit frames, cancel windows and paired-kill sync points stay hand-authored and tuned in tens of milliseconds (For Honor's 33 ms shift). Kimodo should be used for body motion around an authored combat skeleton of keyframes, not as the author of the attack.
