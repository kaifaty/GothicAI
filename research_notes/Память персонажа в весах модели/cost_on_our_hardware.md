# Cost and mechanics of per-NPC LoRA adapters and KV artifacts for Gemma 4 12B (QAT Q4_0 GGUF, llama-server, one RTX 3090), as of 2026-11-04

Conventions: "[Source]" = fetched and read; "(arithmetic)" = my own calculation from cited numbers; "(estimate)" = my reasoning, no direct measurement found. Statements about llama.cpp behaviour marked "(source, master)" come from reading the files on `master` on 2026-11-04 (the files are cited with links). Our build is "from source", so the commit may differ and each such claim should be re-checked against our checkout (`grep -n lora tools/server/server-context.cpp`).

Model facts used for arithmetic, from the `unsloth/gemma-4-12b-it` config (a mirror of Google's config): 48 layers, 40 sliding-window layers (window 1024, 8 KV heads, head_dim 256) and 8 full-attention layers (1 global KV head, global head_dim 512, `attention_k_eq_v: true`), hidden 3840, FFN 15360, vocab 262,144, `num_kv_shared_layers: 0` — [HF config](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/gemma-4-12b-it/raw/main/config.json). Google's docs list Gemma 4 sizes E2B, E4B, 12B, 26B A4B, 31B; 12B weights: 26.7 GB BF16 / 13.4 GB SFP8 / 6.7 GB Q4_0 (table includes 20% overhead); QAT checkpoints (unquantized, GGUF, w4a16 compressed-tensors) exist for 12B — [Google Gemma docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core).

## 1. Training: VRAM, time, rank and size on a 24 GB card; can it run next to llama-server?

### Takeaway
QLoRA of a 12B Gemma fits a 24 GB card alone (about 10-16 GB, Unsloth) but NOT in the 8 GB that is free next to our running stack (about 16 GB used), and no one has published 3090 throughput for Gemma 12B; first-principles throughput is roughly 200-600 training tokens/s, i.e. about 1-2 h for a ~2M-token nightly pass. An adapter at rank 16 over all linear layers is about 65M parameters = about 131 MB in F16 (about 262 MB in the converter's default F32). Training concurrently with live serving on the same GPU is a known source of inference latency degradation and does not fit our memory.

### Cited Findings
- Unsloth's Gemma 4 guide: "Gemma 4 E2B trains on 8GB VRAM"; E4B 10 GB (E4B LoRA needs 17 GB); 31B QLoRA 22 GB; 26B-A4B LoRA more than 40 GB. The guide has no 12B VRAM row (its intro mentions a "12B" once, no other section does). Claimed speed: about 1.5x faster, about 60% less VRAM than FA2 setups (vendor benchmark). — [Unsloth Gemma 4 guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train)
- Unsloth's example hyperparameters for Gemma 4 text SFT: LoRA r=8, alpha=8, dropout 0, batch 1 x grad-accum 4, lr 2e-4 (2e-5 suggested for long runs), max_seq_length 8192, adamw_8bit. — [Unsloth Gemma 4 guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train)
- Unsloth's minimum QLoRA table: 7.5 GB at 11B, 8.5 GB at 14B (absolute minimum, "sometimes more VRAM is required"). — [Unsloth requirements](https://docs.unsloth.ai/get-started/beginner-start-here/unsloth-requirements)
- Practitioner guide for Gemma 3 12B (same text config as the Gemma 4 12B above): QLoRA with Unsloth, ~14 GB peak at seq 2048, batch 1, grad-accum 16; reduce seq length or enable gradient checkpointing on OOM. A single, non-verified guide. — [soup-cli guide](https://soup-cli.vercel.app/docs/fine-tune-gemma-3-qlora)
- Google's own docs give no fine-tuning memory figures ("drastically higher than for standard inference"). — [Google Gemma docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core)
- Unsloth Gemma 4 pitfalls: E2B/E4B share KV across layers so `use_cache=False` (forced by some QLoRA/gradient-checkpointing setups) gives garbage logits (fixed by Unsloth); loss values of 13-15 are "normal" for E2B/E4B, 1-3 for 26B/31B; MoE QLoRA "not recommended"; if the GGUF export misbehaves, the usual cause is a chat-template/EOS mismatch. — [Unsloth Gemma 4 guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train)
- Throughput anecdote (vendor, weak): a GPU-hosting vendor page lists about 48 minutes per 1,000 examples for Qwen 2.5 14B QLoRA (r=16) on an RTX 3090, about 4 h per 5,000 examples; no sequence length or batch size stated. — [GigaGPU fine-tuning time by GPU](https://gigagpu.com/fine-tuning-time-by-gpu/) (the search tool attributed this loosely to the page; treat as an anecdote). The same vendor's 4090 numbers (about 16k tokens/s for 14B QLoRA with Unsloth) are garbled and not usable for a 3090.
- I searched for measured Gemma-12B-on-3090 QLoRA tokens/s (Unsloth/TRL) and found none; results were inference speeds only.
- LoRA compute: LoRA needs "slightly more than 2/3" of full fine-tuning FLOPs per pass (2N^2+6NR vs 3N^2); apply LoRA to all layers, MLP matters most (attention-only underperforms MLP-only at similar parameter counts); optimal LR for LoRA is about 10x the full-fine-tuning LR; LoRA tolerates large batches worse than full fine-tuning; LoRA matches full fine-tuning on small-to-medium SFT sets. — [Thinking Machines, LoRA Without Regret](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)
- Concurrency: research on co-locating inference and LoRA fine-tuning on one GPU reports that co-location "can significantly degrade inference performance" and needs token-level scheduling or preemption of the training job to meet latency targets; no generic measured numbers were found. — [FlexLLM, arXiv 2402.18789](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18789v3)
- Our stack today: about 16 GB of 24 GB used, so about 8 GB free; the machine is shared with CI (facts given in the assignment).

### Inferences
- Adapter size (arithmetic, Gemma 3/4-12B dims, LoRA params per rank unit per layer: q 7,936 + k 5,888 + v 5,888 + o 7,936 + gate/up/down 3 x 19,200 = 85,248; x48 layers = 4.09M per unit of rank): r=8 -> 32.7M params, 65 MB F16; r=16 -> 65.5M, 131 MB; r=32 -> 131M, 262 MB; r=64 -> 524 MB. Attention-only r=16: about 21M params, 42 MB. MLP-only r=16: about 44M, 88 MB. `convert_lora_to_gguf.py` defaults to `--outtype f32` (choices f32/f16/bf16/q8_0/auto), so convert with `--outtype f16` or `bf16` to halve the file (see section 2 for the script).
- Training throughput (estimate): QLoRA with gradient checkpointing is about 6N FLOPs/token = about 70 GFLOP/token for N=12B (4N without recompute). The 3090's dense FP16/BF16 tensor peak with FP32 accumulate is about 71 TFLOPS (from my memory of the NVIDIA spec, not fetched). At 30-40% utilisation (21-28 TFLOPS; 4-bit dequant makes this optimistic) that is about 300-600 tokens/s; the vendor anecdote (48 min/1k examples at 14B) implies about 180-350 tokens/s if an example is 512-1024 tokens. Working range: 200-600 training tokens/s. Needs a real measurement before any plan depends on it.
- Time per pass (estimate): 1M training tokens takes about 30-80 min; a 2M-token nightly pass (see section 5) takes about 1-2.5 h per NPC, plus a minute or two to load the 4-bit base. A smaller base (E4B, 10 GB; E2B, 8 GB) is about 3-6x cheaper per token but is a different model than the one we serve, so its adapter cannot be applied to the 12B.
- Base for training: BF16 12B weights are 24+ GB, so a 3090 cannot do BF16 LoRA of the 12B; the only on-card route is QLoRA (NF4 via bitsandbytes) on Google's `-unquantized` QAT checkpoint. The NF4 grid is a different 4-bit rounding than the Q4_0 grid the model was QAT-trained for, so the adapter is trained against a slightly different base than the one it will run on (see section 2).
- Concurrent training does not fit: QLoRA 12B needs about 10-16 GB vs about 8 GB free, with CI also on the card; the big logits tensor (vocab 262,144) is a known memory hog for Gemma training (my knowledge, not sourced here). Even if it fit, a training process time-slices the same SMs as decoding; decode requests at 0.3-0.4 s first-word targets would see multiples of that during training steps (estimate; FlexLLM above supports the direction, not the size). Practical options: train while the game is not generating (pause/stop the other models), or on another machine (Ryzen RTX 3080 10 GB is too small for 12B QLoRA at 10-16 GB; dev-ubuntu is the same card as serving). On a player's 16 GB GPU, the full stack already uses about 16 GB, so training cannot be concurrent there at all.

### Gaps
- No measured tokens/s or seconds/step for Gemma 3/4 12B QLoRA on a 3090 (Unsloth, TRL, Axolotl). Needs a 10-minute benchmark on dev-ubuntu with the real sequence lengths.
- No published 12B row in Unsloth's Gemma 4 VRAM table; the 10-16 GB range is interpolated from neighbours and one Gemma 3 guide.
- No data on TRL/PEFT/Axolotl or llama.cpp's own `finetune` for Gemma 4 (I did not find llama.cpp finetune support evidence; not researched in depth).
- No measured interference numbers for training next to llama-server on one GPU.

## 2. The quantisation gap: adapters trained on BF16/QLoRA base applied to Google's QAT Q4_0 GGUF; llama.cpp support for Gemma 3/4 LoRA; conversion

### Takeaway
llama.cpp does support LoRA for Gemma 3 and Gemma 4 (the converter reuses the model classes; a Gemma 4 GGUF LoRA exists on HF), and applies the adapter unmerged on top of the quantised weights at run time, which avoids re-quantisation loss. But Google's Q4_0 GGUF in llama.cpp is itself a noisy copy of the QAT model (12B: mean KLD 0.507, top-1 74% vs the BF16 QAT model; Unsloth's rebuild: 0.133 and 89%), and I found NO published measurement of how well an adapter trained on BF16/NF4 transfers to that Q4_0 base; it must be measured.

### Cited Findings
- llama.cpp `conversion/gemma.py` registers `Gemma3ForCausalLM`/`Gemma3ForConditionalGeneration` (Gemma3Model), `Gemma3n...`, and `Gemma4ForConditionalGeneration`/`Gemma4ForCausalLM` (Gemma4Model, subclass of Gemma3Model). `convert_lora_to_gguf.py` looks up the model class from the adapter's base model, so Gemma 3/4 text adapters are convertible. — [conversion/gemma.py](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/conversion/gemma.py), [convert_lora_to_gguf.py](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/convert_lora_to_gguf.py) (source, master)
- A Gemma 4 LoRA in GGUF with architecture `gemma4` exists on HF (E2B-based "Gemopus"), loaded as base GGUF plus the adapter file. — [Hashhasapi/Gemopus-v1-e2b](https://huggingface.co/Hashhasapi/Gemopus-v1-e2b)
- Known converter pitfalls: an adapter trained on a Gemma 3 multimodal model failed with `Can not map tensor 'model.vision_tower...'` — [zenn write-up](https://zenn.dev/akakagerengoku/articles/5561bf474b59fa); for Gemma 2, an `lm_head` adapter tensor was silently skipped and so not applied — [llama.cpp issue #9065](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/9065). The converter raises an error when `lm_head` is in the adapter but ignored in the base (tied embeddings). — [convert_lora_to_gguf.py](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/convert_lora_to_gguf.py) (source, master)
- `convert_lora_to_gguf.py --outtype` choices are f32, f16, bf16, q8_0, auto; default f32. — [convert_lora_to_gguf.py](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/convert_lora_to_gguf.py) (source, master)
- Runtime application is unmerged: for each adapted weight the graph computes `W x` (quantised) plus `scale * B(A x)` with the adapter tensors, `scale = adapter_scale * alpha / rank`; adapter tensors are allocated in the same backend buffer type as the layer they belong to (VRAM for offloaded layers). — [llama-graph.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/src/llama-graph.cpp), [llama-adapter.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/src/llama-adapter.cpp) (source, master)
- Official guidance from the original 2023 PR: adding LoRA deltas to an already-quantised model "is most likely very bad for quality" and the suggested workaround then was an f16 base (`--lora-base`) merged then quantised; later work supports patching quantised models and one test reported acceptable q4_0 perplexity (secondary summary). — [llama.cpp PR #820](https://github.com/ggerganov/llama.cpp/pull/820)
- The GGUF-my-LoRA announcement shows adapters (F16) loaded onto a Q4_K_M base with `--lora`/`--lora-scaled`; no quality numbers. — [llama.cpp discussion #10123](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/10123)
- Unsloth on Gemma 4 QAT: converting Google's QAT Q4_0 to llama.cpp Q4_0 is lossy because "llama.cpp uses F16 scales, whilst QAT BF16 uses BF16 scales"; byte-for-byte match of the naive conversion to the QAT BF16 weights: 24.77% vs 99.96% for Unsloth's. 12B, vs the BF16 QAT model (presumably the reference): Google-style Q4_0 6.98 GB, mean KLD 0.50702, 99.9% KLD 14.73, top-1 74.08%; Unsloth 6.72 GB, mean KLD 0.13288, 99.9% KLD 9.27, top-1 88.76%. Other sizes for comparison: E4B Q4_0 0.03778 vs Unsloth 0.00121; 31B Q4_0 0.09349 vs 0.01403. — [Unsloth Gemma 4 QAT](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/qat)
- llama.cpp discussion (2026-08-09) on optional BF16 scales for Q4_0: same F16-scale diagnosis, reports of Q4_0 QAT 26B being worse than regular Q4_K; marked "partially addressed" by PR #29675, which on inspection only adds BF16 element-wise ops (merged 2026-09-30) — not a fix of the Q4_0 scale format. — [discussion #26788](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/26788), [PR #29675](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/29675)
- Unsloth's training docs/QAT page do not say how to LoRA a QAT checkpoint and export it; the Gemma 4 guide shows `save_pretrained_gguf` with q4_k_m/q8_0/f16 (merged export), not Q4_0 and not adapter-only. — [Unsloth Gemma 4 guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train), [Unsloth QAT](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/qat)
- I searched for a measurement of "LoRA trained on fp16/bf16 applied to quantised GGUF vs merge-and-requantise" and found none; guides only describe the merge, convert to F16 GGUF, quantise path. — [Sunbird quantisation guide](https://docs.sunbird.ai/sunflower/quantization), [markaicode guide](https://www.markaicode.com/gguf-quantization-after-fine-tuning-llama-cpp/)

### Inferences
- Running the adapter unmerged is the right choice for us: a merge would round the small delta into a 4-bit grid, and the runtime path keeps the delta in F16/F32. The cost is extra ops at run time (section 3).
- Two stacked mismatches: (a) training base (NF4 of BF16 QAT, or BF16 with LoRA only on a smaller model) vs serving base (Google Q4_0 in llama.cpp, KLD 0.5 from the QAT BF16 model); (b) the adapter was fitted to compensate nothing about (a). Because the LoRA delta is additive in activation space and small, the typical expectation is that it mostly transfers, but a mean KLD of 0.5 on 12B suggests the base already shifts behaviour noticeably (first-word persona and Russian fluency should be tested). This is an inference, not a reported result.
- Options to close the gap, in rising effort: (1) check which Q4_0 GGUF we serve and whether Unsloth's UD-Q4_K_XL (KLD 0.133, 6.72 GB, almost the same size) is acceptable instead, since the serving-side error is then four times smaller; (2) evaluate the adapter on the actual served GGUF with a held-out recall/persona set, not on the HF model; (3) train with the dequantised Q4_0 weights as base (loading GGUF into transformers dequantises it; support for Gemma 4 is unverified) or on the `-w4a16-ct` compressed-tensors checkpoint (unverified whether it is trainable with PEFT), so the adapter sees the same grid.
- Convert with `--outtype f16`; target only text-model modules (exclude vision/audio towers, `embed_tokens` and `lm_head`) to avoid the two reported converter failures.

### Gaps
- No measurement anywhere of adapter transfer from BF16/NF4 to Google's Q4_0 Gemma 4 GGUF, nor of a LoRA trained on the QAT checkpoint. The size of the effect for the 12B is unknown.
- Not verified: end-to-end `convert_lora_to_gguf.py` on a Gemma 4 12B PEFT adapter (the 12B has a vision tower in the HF checkpoint; the 12B is described by Google as an "encoder free" multimodal model, which may change tensor names).
- Not verified: whether the reference of Unsloth's KLD table is the BF16 QAT model (the page implies it but does not state it).

## 3. Serving: multi-LoRA in llama-server, per-request switching, slots, KV/prompt cache interaction

### Takeaway
Adapters can only be loaded at server start (`--lora`); at run time you can set global scales (`POST /lora-adapters`) or pass a per-request `lora` array of `{id, scale}`. Different slots can run different adapters simultaneously but are never batched together, a change of adapter on a slot clears that slot's prompt cache, and every adapter switch between batches triggers a scheduler re-reserve. So a per-NPC adapter is workable only if each NPC keeps its own slot with a fixed adapter; hot-adding a newly trained adapter needs a server restart.

### Cited Findings
- Server flags: `--lora FNAME[,FNAME]` (comma-separated or repeated), `--lora-scaled FNAME:SCALE`, `--lora-init-without-apply` ("load LoRA adapters without applying them (apply later via POST /lora-adapters)"). `GET /lora-adapters` lists adapters with ids and scales; `POST /lora-adapters` "sets the global scale"; per-request `lora` in `/completion` overrides it; adapters missing from the request list get scale 0.0. README: "requests with different LoRA configurations will not be batched together". — [server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md)
- PR "server : allow using LoRA adapters per-request" (#10994) introduced the per-request field, `slot.can_batch_with`, and the warning that different configs "may result in performance degradation"; it closed issue #10377, whose motivation was that global hot-swap is only safe when all slots are idle. Issue #10374 describes the race of one request's scale change disrupting a request in flight. — [PR #10994](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/10994), [issue #10377](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/10377), [issue #10374](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/10374)
- Adapters are only ever added at start-up: `--lora`/`--lora-scaled` append to `params.lora_adapters`; the only runtime route (`SERVER_TASK_TYPE_SET_LORA`) rebuilds the list with new scales for the already-loaded adapters (id = index in the list). No endpoint loads or unloads an adapter file. — [common/arg.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/common/arg.cpp), [server-context.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/server-context.cpp) (source, master)
- No hard limit on the number of loaded adapters in the code I read; only adapters with non-zero scale enter the compute graph (`set_adapters_lora` skips zero scales; `build_lora_mm` loops over active ones), so loaded-but-unused adapters cost VRAM, not compute. Per active adapter and per adapted weight the graph adds 2 `mul_mat` + 1 `scale` + 1 `add`. — [llama-context.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/src/llama-context.cpp), [llama-graph.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/src/llama-graph.cpp) (source, master)
- Each slot has its own `lora` vector; `can_batch_with` requires `are_lora_equal(lora, other.lora)` (same adapter pointers and scales); before each decode the server calls `common_set_adapter_lora(ctx, slot_batched->lora)` once per batch. If the set is unchanged the call returns early; otherwise the context's adapter map is rebuilt and `sched_need_reserve = true`, which makes the next decode re-run `sched_reserve()` (logs "reserve took X ms"). — [server-context.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/server-context.cpp), [llama-context.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/src/llama-context.cpp) (source, master)
- Cache validity: in `launch_slot_with_task`, when a request carries a non-empty `lora` that differs from the slot's current `lora`, the server calls `lora_should_clear_cache` and for normal (non-activated) LoRA does `slot.prompt.clear()` — the slot's cached prefix is dropped. Only aLoRA (activated LoRA, invocation-token based) keeps the cache. — [server-context.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/server-context.cpp), [server-common.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/server-common.cpp) (source, master)
- A request WITHOUT a `lora` field (or with an empty array, which is treated as "not specified") sets `slot.lora = params_base.lora_adapters` (the global state) and does NOT clear the cache. — [server-context.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/server-context.cpp) (source, master)
- The host-RAM prompt cache entry (`server_prompt`) and the slot save file hold only tokens and context checkpoints; there is no adapter identity in them. — [server-task.h](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/server-task.h) (source, master)
- The README itself says nothing about how LoRA and the prompt cache interact (only the batching note); the PR description likewise. — [server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md), [PR #10994](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/10994)

### Inferences
- Yes, a cached prefix computed with adapter A is invalid for adapter B (K/V of every adapted layer depend on the adapter). The server handles the explicit per-request case by clearing the slot cache, so a slot that alternates adapters re-prefills its whole persona + history each time (8-16k tokens), destroying the 0.3-0.4 s first-word design. Design rule: one NPC = one slot = one fixed adapter set; never change a pinned slot's adapter between turns.
- Hazards seen in the code (not tested): (1) a no-`lora` request after an adapter request on the same slot keeps the stale prefix; always send an explicit list, and to mean "base model" send `[{"id":0,"scale":0.0}]` rather than `[]`; (2) `--cache-ram`'s host cache and slot save files are not adapter-aware, so restoring a file saved under adapter A into a slot later run with adapter B can reuse a stale prefix: put the adapter hash in the file name and check it before restore. We already run `--no-cache-idle-slots`, which avoids part of the host-cache path but not the `--cache-ram` default (8192 MiB) save/load on slot reassignment.
- Per-token cost of an active adapter (arithmetic from the graph code): +4 graph nodes per adapted weight x 7 weights x 48 layers = about 1,344 extra nodes per forward pass, plus reading the adapter (131 MB at r=16, about 2% of the 6.7 GB base) per decode step. The bandwidth share is small; the kernel-count overhead on a 10-15 ms decode step is the unknown. I found no published measurement of `--lora` overhead on a quantised base; run `llama-bench` with and without `--lora` on our build.
- Multi-adapter cost (estimate): when two slots with different adapters are active, each iteration alternates adapter sets, and each alternation triggers `sched_reserve()` (a full worst-case graph reservation for a 48-layer model). It is logged, so measurable, but I have no number; it could be tens of ms per switch and kills batching between those NPCs. Only one NPC talking at a time avoids it.
- VRAM per adapter equals its file size (F16): r=16 all-linear 131 MB, r=32 262 MB. Three pinned NPC adapters at r=16 = about 0.4 GB; 50 resident NPC adapters = 6.5 GB, impossible on 16 GB cards. Adapters for NPCs not near the player cannot be paged in without restarting the server (loaded only at start-up). A small upstream patch (load/unload endpoint) or restart-on-load-game is needed for "train in the background, then load next to the running model". Restart cost = reload of the 6.7 GB model (page-cache dependent) + re-prewarm of 3 slots: not measured.
- Rollback with saves: an adapter is a file of 65-260 MB, so save-game rollback = keep per-save immutable adapter files (content hash in the name) and point the NPC to the older one; the memory log already rolls back exactly, so the weights must be derived data that can always be rebuilt from the log. A rolled-back adapter has to be loaded, which again means start-up loading.

### Gaps
- No documented statement on cache validity beyond the code; behaviour in our build must be verified with a test (same slot, adapter A request then no-lora request with an identical prompt; check `n_prompt_tokens_cache` in the response/timings).
- No measured switch/reserve latency, no measured decode-speed penalty for `--lora` on Gemma 4, no interaction data with `--swa-full` off or with context checkpoints.
- Did not read the router/multi-model mode of the new llama-server (`models_routes`), which might support per-model adapter sets.

## 4. KV/prompt state as a per-NPC memory artifact: slot save/restore, size and time; rollback

### Takeaway
`POST /slots/{id}?action=save|restore` (needs `--slot-save-path`) writes the slot's KV cells, token list and context checkpoints to one file and restores them; it is a cache of a prefix, not memory by itself, but it is cheap and exactly reproducible per save. For Gemma 4 12B with q8_0 KV, a 16k-token slot is about 0.3 GB without `--swa-full` and about 3 GB with it (arithmetic), and restore time is dominated by disk (about 0.1-0.2 s for 0.3 GB, about 1 s for 3 GB, estimate).

### Cited Findings
- README: `--slot-save-path PATH`; `POST /slots/{id_slot}?action=save` returns `n_saved`, `n_written`, `timings.save_ms`; `action=restore` returns `n_restored`, `n_read`, `timings.restore_ms`; `action=erase`. — [server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md)
- Implementation: save writes `llama_state_seq_save_file` (KV for that sequence plus the token list) and then appends the slot's context checkpoints (`--ctx-checkpoints`, default 32 per slot; `--checkpoint-min-step` default 8192 tokens) and draft state to the same file; a busy slot defers the task; restore rebuilds the slot's prompt from the file and refuses a prompt longer than the slot context. The file does not record the LoRA set. — [server-context.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/server-context.cpp) (source, master); options in [server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md)
- Measured example (Qwen2.5-7B Q4_K_M on an Apple M3 Max, one build, F16-sized KV): about 56.9 KB of KV per token; snapshots 0.20 GB at about 3.7k tokens and 0.82 GB at about 15.3k tokens; restores 0.04 s and 0.19 s at about 4.4-5.0 GB/s. — [hearth-llm README](https://pypi.org/project/hearth-llm/)
- Restart-proof KV: a patched build restoring a 40k-token prefix after reboot cut time-to-first-token from about 155 s to about 6 s on a GTX 1080 8 GB (single user, not upstream). — [discussion #29885](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/29885)
- A restored file must match the flash-attention and KV-cache type settings of the saving run. — [stillwarm README](https://pypi.org/project/stillwarm/)
- Rolling back SWA models without `--swa-full`: we already know it cannot roll back beyond about the window (assignment); the SWA cache is sized to the window plus batch, the README flag text is "use full-size SWA cache (default: false)". — [server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md)

### Inferences
- KV bytes per token for Gemma 4 12B, q8_0 (34 bytes per 32 elements = 1.0625 B/element), arithmetic from the config: sliding layers 40 x 8 heads x 256 x 2 (K+V) = 163,840 elements = about 170 KiB/token; global layers 8 x 1 x 512 x 2 = 8,192 elements = about 8.5 KiB/token (halve if llama.cpp exploits `attention_k_eq_v`; unverified).
- File size, 16,384-token slot (arithmetic): without `--swa-full` the sliding cache keeps only about the last 1,024 tokens (plus batch padding) = about 178 MB, the global layers hold all 16k = about 143 MB, total about 320 MB (about 300 MiB), plus context checkpoints (each one stores a window-sized SWA blob; count depends on the 8,192-token spacing; up to a few hundred MB more). With `--swa-full`: 16,384 x 174 KB + 143 MB = about 3.0 GB. A 4k-token slot: about 215 MB without, about 750 MB with `--swa-full`. The endpoint returns `n_bytes`/`n_written`, so the real number is one call away.
- Restore time (estimate): at NVMe/page-cache speeds of 3-5 GB/s (cf. the 4.4-5.0 GB/s M3 Max measurement above) 0.3 GB restores in about 0.1 s and 3 GB in about 0.6-1 s, plus host-to-device copy at PCIe speed (not the bottleneck). Compared to re-prefilling 16k tokens of a 12B Q4_0 (seconds), this is a large saving, but it only reproduces the prefix we already prewarm today.
- As a "memory artifact" for rollback: one immutable file per (save, NPC) keyed by model hash, KV type, FA flag and adapter hash gives exact rollback for free and ties to the append-only log. 20 NPCs x 0.3-0.7 GB = 6-14 GB per save without `--swa-full`: acceptable on disk, heavy if saves are frequent. It does not store knowledge beyond the text already in the prompt, so its value is latency, not recall.

### Gaps
- No measured save/restore size or time for a Gemma 4 12B slot with q8_0 KV on our build; the SWA + checkpoint file layout was not measured, so the 0.3 GB figure may be off by 2x.
- Not verified how llama.cpp stores K and V for the `attention_k_eq_v` global layers.

## 5. Data side: examples per character, and how long a "sleep" training pass takes

### Takeaway
Raw capacity is not the limit (an r=16 adapter has about 65M parameters; a few hundred memories are a few thousand bits); recall robustness is. Literature shows near-perfect recall of 500 trained facts on an 8B model with a rank-1 LoRA when the same questions are asked, but extraction under new phrasing needs diverse augmentation (about 5-10 variants per fact), and each extra paraphrase costs general-ability retention. Plan on roughly 10 variants x 3-10 epochs per memory plus replay data: 1-2.5 h of 3090 time per NPC per night at several hundred memories (estimate).

### Cited Findings
- LoRA knowledge injection into Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (rank 1, alpha 2, MLP projections only, lr 1e-3, batch 16, 10 epochs; 1 to 3,000 unknown facts): reliability (recall of the trained questions) stays near 1.0 up to 500 facts without extras and 0.98 at 3,000; "for 3,000 unknown samples 10 epochs is not enough"; with 3,000 facts plus 10 highly-known samples per fact it falls to 0.48, with 10 paraphrases per fact it is 0.99. Train and test overlap by design, so this measures memorisation, not generalisation to new phrasings. — [Pletenev et al., arXiv 2502.14502](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14502v2)
- Same paper, side effects: MMLU 0.677 baseline -> 0.649 at 500 facts, 0.579 at 3,000 facts, 0.441 at 3,000 facts + 10 paraphrases; TruthfulQA MC1 0.382 -> 0.343 at 500 facts + 10 paraphrases; "answer collapse" (one answer given 9,393 times, unique answers 48,136 -> 22,804 for 10 facts); refusals drop from about 15% to about 0. — [Pletenev et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14502v2)
- Knowledge must be "sufficiently augmented" or it is stored but not extractable: without augmentation QA accuracy after fine-tuning can be 0%; with 5 diverse biography entries per person plus sentence shuffling accuracy rose from 9.7% to 96.6% (synthetic biographies, pretraining from scratch, up to 1,350 exposures per individual: a different regime than LoRA). — [Allen-Zhu and Li, Physics of LMs 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.14316v3), [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14316)
- Capacity: models "can and only can store 2 bits of knowledge per parameter, even when quantized to int8". — [Allen-Zhu and Li, Physics of LMs 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405)
- Fine-tuning on facts the model does not know: those examples are learned significantly slower than known ones, and once learned they linearly increase hallucination; early stopping and filtering mitigate it. — [Gekhman et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.444)
- LoRA "learns less and forgets less" than full fine-tuning: it underperforms in standard low-rank settings on code/math, full fine-tuning learns perturbations with 10-100x higher rank, and LoRA keeps out-of-domain performance better and generations more diverse. — [Biderman et al., arXiv 2405.09673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673)
- LoRA matches full fine-tuning on small-to-medium SFT sets; underperforms once the dataset exceeds its capacity; attention-only LoRA is worse than MLP-only. — [Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)

### Inferences
- Per NPC per day (estimate, my assumptions): 100-300 new memories, each turned into about 10 training samples (statement, several question-answer forms, in-persona dialogue lines, Russian) of about 100-150 tokens = 0.1-0.5M tokens per epoch; 3-5 epochs and an equal volume of replay (persona dialogue, earlier memories) gives about 0.6-3M tokens. At 200-600 tokens/s this is 20 min to 4 h per NPC per night; 3 active NPCs would take most of a night on one 3090, and a world of 50 NPCs is out of reach without a per-NPC budget (train only the NPCs whose memory changed).
- Capacity (arithmetic): r=16 all-linear = 65M parameters, at the 2-bits/parameter upper bound about 130M bits; 300 memories x about 100 bits is about 30k bits. So rank can stay small (r=8-16), and the constraint is the interference and forgetting, not storage.
- Because the literature measures recall under the training phrasing, the number to plan for is the variants: with only the canonical sentence expect poor extraction under free-form NPC dialogue; budget about 5-10 diverse variants per memory (Allen-Zhu's 5; Pletenev's 10), and test recall with unseen phrasings and in Russian.
- Retrieval over the existing SQLite log gives exact rollback and no forgetting; weights add value only for behaviour/style/relationship tone and for facts needed without retrieval latency. The cost side above should be set against that (not in this note's scope).

### Gaps
- No concrete guidance for Gemma, for Russian, or for LoRA at r=16-64 with free-form dialogue recall; the studies use English QA on Llama-3.1-8B and synthetic biographies.
- No measured "minutes per 1k examples" for Gemma 4 12B; the 20 min-4 h range is built on the throughput estimate in section 1.
- Allen-Zhu's exposures figure (about 100 vs 1,000 for 2 bits/parameter) was not retrievable from the pages fetched.

## 6. Risks reported by practitioners: forgetting of instruction-following/persona, overfitting to repeated phrases, quality loss in other languages (Russian)

### Takeaway
Measured effects exist for general-ability loss (MMLU -0.03 to -0.24 at 500-3,000 facts) and for answer collapse and lost refusal calibration; LoRA forgets less than full fine-tuning but not zero. For persona/instruction-following and for Russian specifically I found no direct measurement, so those are unquantified risks to test, not to assume.

### Cited Findings
- General-ability drop after fact injection (Llama-3.1-8B, rank-1 LoRA): MMLU 0.677 -> 0.615 at 10 facts (noisy), 0.649 at 500, 0.579 at 3,000, 0.441 at 3,000 + 10 paraphrases; "adding 10 highly-known or paraphrased samples leads to a significant drop in accuracy"; paraphrase-trained models disrupt truthfulness less. — [Pletenev et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14502v2)
- Overfitting to repeated outputs: the fine-tuned model "tends to regress to few overrepresented answers" (one answer in 9,393 cases) and refusals fall from about 15% to about 0 (calibration of "I don't know" lost). — [Pletenev et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14502v2)
- Fine-tuning on unknown facts raises hallucination linearly once they are learned; early stopping helps. — [Gekhman et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.444)
- LoRA mitigates forgetting more than weight decay/dropout and keeps generations more diverse than full fine-tuning; LoRA learns less. — [Biderman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673)
- LoRA is less tolerant of large batch sizes (loss penalty rises with batch size and is not fixed by rank), and needs about 10x the full fine-tuning learning rate (about 15x for runs under about 100 steps, anecdotal). — [Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)
- Export mismatches show up as quality loss: Unsloth says to use the same chat template and EOS as in training; for reasoning behaviour mix reasoning and direct examples (at least 75% reasoning) — we run `--reasoning-budget 0`, so the template/EOS point is the relevant one. Unsloth states Gemma 4 supports 140 languages (marketing, not a Russian-retention measurement). — [Unsloth Gemma 4 guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train)
- Serving-side: Q4_0 GGUF of the 12B has mean KLD 0.507 and top-1 74% vs the QAT BF16 model; a Q4_0 export can degrade accuracy independent of any adapter. — [Unsloth Gemma 4 QAT](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/qat)

### Inferences
- Retention recipe (estimate from the above): replay a mix of the NPC's original persona dialogue and neutral Russian chat, keep few epochs and early stop on a held-out set, keep rank modest, use a low scale at serving (the server accepts `scale` per request, e.g. 0.5-0.8) as a safety lever for style drift, and treat a drop in general Russian fluency as an acceptance criterion measured on the served GGUF.
- Repeated-phrase overfit is a direct risk for NPC lines: if many training samples share an opening or phrasing, expect the adapter to push those phrases into unrelated replies. Vary surface forms and cap repeats per phrase.
- Persona stability is a bigger problem when adapters are retrained nightly from the previous adapter (compounding drift): retrain from the base model on the full memory set each time, or keep the base adapter fixed and train a small delta, so rollback and determinism are clean.

### Gaps
- No source measuring LoRA fine-tuning effects on Russian dialogue quality, persona consistency or instruction-following for Gemma 3/4 (searches returned English QA studies only).
- No practitioner reports (r/LocalLLaMA etc.) of per-character adapters for game NPCs were found in this pass.
- No data on Gemma 4 12B specifically under QLoRA of the QAT checkpoint (bitsandbytes NF4 of a QAT model is untested in what I found).
