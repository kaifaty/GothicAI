# Storing new facts and memories in LLM weights vs. keeping them outside the model (evidence sheet, as of 2026-11-04)

> Reading notes. (1) Most numbers below were pulled from arXiv HTML pages by a summarising fetch tool, not read by eye; spot-check any number before it goes into a public doc. Known inconsistencies in the sources are flagged inline. (2) Tags: [AUTHOR] = the method's own authors report it; [INDEP] = a third party evaluated someone else's method or ran an analysis; [PREPRINT] = no venue visible on the page I read; [SECONDARY] = number comes from a search-result summary or review site, the paper itself was not opened. (3) Almost all evidence is about world facts (Wikipedia/news/synthetic biographies), not about "who said what to whom, and was it a lie" in a conversation. That gap is itself the main finding.

## Fine-tuning vs. retrieval for new facts: how many examples, paraphrases and epochs make a fact recallable, and how do recall rates compare with RAG?

### Takeaway
Across independent and author studies, putting a fact into weights is reliably worse than putting it in context: it needs many varied rewrites of each fact (roughly 10-30 or more), several epochs, and still usually trails RAG on the same facts, while recall of the fact in a different direction or form (reverse, multi-hop, composition) can stay near zero. The best weight-injection results of 2024-2026 (self-distillation, EntiGraph, SEAL) approach but rarely beat RAG, and they cost GPU hours per small corpus.

### Cited Findings
**Fine-tuning vs RAG, head to head**
- Ovadia et al. (EMNLP 2024), Mistral-7B / Llama2-7B / Orca2-7B, 910 current-events questions (Aug-Nov 2023, post-cutoff), unsupervised fine-tuning up to 5 epochs: Mistral base 0.481, base+RAG 0.875, fine-tuned on original text 0.504, fine-tuned with paraphrases 0.588, fine-tuned+RAG 0.810 (FT-par+RAG 0.830). Llama2-7B: base 0.353, RAG 0.585, FT 0.219 (below base), FT-par 0.392. Orca2-7B: base 0.456, RAG 0.876, FT 0.511, FT-par 0.566. RAG beat fine-tuning on every model; RAG+FT was not better than RAG alone on current events. [AUTHOR] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05934); venue per [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.15.pdf)
- Same paper: each chunk was paraphrased 10 times for the "FT-par" arm; accuracy rose monotonically with the number of paraphrases for all three models (Figure 4, exact values not given in text); authors' hypothesis is that facts must be seen in many forms to be learned. On MMLU subjects fine-tuning gave inconsistent small gains (e.g. Mistral anatomy 0.556 base, 0.570 fine-tuned, 0.681 RAG). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05934)
- Gekhman et al. (EMNLP 2024, Google/Technion), closed-book QA on EntityQuestions, 6,142 fine-tuning examples, split 24% HighlyKnown / 23% MaybeKnown / 17% WeaklyKnown / 36% Unknown: examples carrying facts the model did not know were fitted much more slowly than known ones; once fitted they raised hallucination roughly linearly (fit: accuracy ≈ 36.9 + 7.3 x fraction of Known fitted - 8.3 x fraction of Unknown fitted, R²=0.86). Training on Unknown-only examples: 37.5 test accuracy with early stopping but 25.8 at convergence; natural mix 43.5 early-stop vs 41.8 at convergence. Authors' conclusion: models mostly acquire facts in pretraining; fine-tuning teaches use of existing knowledge. [AUTHOR; independent of the RAG-vs-FT question] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05904), [abstract page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05904). Flag: one fetch reported the base model as PaLM 2-S, my recollection is PaLM 2-M, the abstract page does not say; verify before citing the model size.
- Self-distillation ("prompt distillation", LoRA student matches the same model's logits when the document is in its prompt), closed-book accuracy on SQuADShifts subsets Amazon/New Wiki/NYT/Reddit, Llama-3-8B-Instruct: base 22.1/61.2/38.2/20.8; plain SFT on generated QA 75.9/89.5/87.5/69.8; prompt distillation 86.1/94.4/93.6/79.5; base+RAG 86.3/95.6/96.3/78.6; PD+RAG 88.5/96.7/96.9/77.8. HotpotQA (multi-hop, trained only on single-hop questions): base 53.0, SFT 63.3, PD 70.0 (73.7 with 100/50 questions), base+RAG 79.5, PD+RAG 81.7. Cost: 30 generated training questions per test question (200 for the XL variant), the 8B run took about 24 h on one GPU, needs teacher logits. [AUTHOR, preprint] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14964)
- EntiGraph / synthetic continued pretraining: 265 QuALITY books (about 1.3M tokens) expanded to 455M synthetic tokens (gpt-4-turbo) and used to continue-pretrain Llama 3 8B Base. Closed-book accuracy 39.49% base -> 56.22% (GPT-4 closed-book 51.30%); log-linear scaling with synthetic tokens; with RAG 60.35% (base) vs 62.60% (EntiGraph CPT); raw-text CPT performed worse than the base model. [AUTHOR] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07431)
- Low-resource knowledge injection (Llama-2-7B-chat, 117 post-cutoff news documents, 468 QA pairs, 1/5/10/20/40 synthetic variations per document, 2 epochs): paraphrase / rephrase-the-web variants improve monotonically with more variations and approach document-level RAG; chunk-level RAG drops noticeably; control-task accuracy falls as variations increase (forgetting). Exact numbers only in figures. [AUTHOR, preprint] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06178)
- SEAL (MIT, NeurIPS 2025 poster): Qwen2.5-7B learns a single passage by generating its own "self-edits" (training data), rewarded with RL (ReSTEM, 2 iterations, 5 self-edits x 50 contexts per iteration); no-context SQuAD accuracy 47.0 vs 39.7 for synthetic data from the base model and 46.3 for GPT-4.1 synthetic data; base accuracy 33.5% is from a secondary review. [AUTHOR] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.10943), [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/118690), [SECONDARY review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/self-adapting-language-models)
- Mecklenburg et al. (Microsoft, 2024) compared token-based vs fact-based scaling of SFT data and benchmarked against RAG; I only saw the abstract (token-based scaling helps but gives uneven coverage of the new facts). — [Microsoft Research page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/injecting-new-knowledge-into-large-language-models-via-supervised-fine-tuning/)

**What LoRA can and cannot take in**
- "LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less" (Llama-2-7B; code and math; LoRA rank 16/64/256 on all modules): continued pretraining on 20B tokens, HumanEval full FT 0.263 vs LoRA r16 0.162 / r64 0.196 / r256 0.224; GSM8K (math CPT) full FT 0.293 vs 0.158 / 0.163 / 0.202. Instruction fine-tuning (about 73-103M tokens) closes the gap only at rank 256 (code peak 0.497 full vs 0.498 r256; r16 0.358). Authors measured that full-FT weight updates have rank 10-100x higher than typical LoRA configs. These are large domain-adaptation runs, not tiny fact insertions. [INDEP of LoRA's inventors; own experiments] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09673)

**Generalisation of what is learned (reversal, manipulation, augmentation)**
- Reversal curse: GPT-3-175B fine-tuned on fictitious "A is B" facts, 30 paraphrases per fact: asked in the trained order, DescriptionToName 96.7% / NameToDescription 50.0%; asked in reverse order 0.1% and 0.0%; log-probability of the correct name was not above a random name's. Same facts given in context: about 100% in both directions. Real celebrities, GPT-4: parent given child 79% vs child given parent 33%. Llama-1 fine-tuned 20 epochs on reversed Q/A: Q->A above 80%, A->Q below 7%. Note: the paper states both 900 documents and 3,030 base facts for Experiment 1 (internal inconsistency). [AUTHOR] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.12288)
- Physics of Language Models 3.1 (ICML 2024), models pretrained from scratch on 100k synthetic biographies: without augmentation, 0% QA accuracy on held-out people despite 99+% first-token memorisation accuracy; single 9.7%; single+permute5 70%; five paraphrased entries per person ("multi5") 41%; multi5+permute 96.6% (mapping of 41% / 96.6% to configs is not fully clear in the fetch); augmenting only a subset ("celebrities") lifted others from 4.4% to 86.8% on bioS. [AUTHOR] — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.14316v3), [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/allen-zhu24a.html)
- Physics of LM 3.2: retrieval of a stored attribute works, but classification/comparison fail without chain-of-thought used in both training and inference, and inverse search ("who has attribute X?") is "virtually 0%, regardless of the prompts"; claimed to hold for GPT-4 too. [AUTHOR] — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14402)
- Physics of LM 3.3 (ICLR 2025): about 2 bits of knowledge per parameter when each fact is seen about 1000 times; with 100 exposures capacity drops to about 1 bit/parameter. Numbers taken from a search-result summary of the paper. [AUTHOR, SECONDARY] — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/26d3c9a66836ded8f34a944f2bfe868e-Abstract-Conference.html), [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2404.05405)
- O'Neill et al. (Qwen3-4B/8B, 247 invented facts, LoRA rank 16 lr 2e-4, 20 sequential writes; preprint v3 Oct 2026): training each fact on the same statement in 2 fixed formats vs on 24 varied examples (paraphrases, Q&A, scenarios, contrasts). After one write, lenient-minus-strict accuracy gap 26-28 pp (repeated) vs 3-4 pp (varied). Raising repeated-statement training to 192 steps did not help application/composition/counterfactual questions; adding 24 recall/paraphrase prompts cut the gap from 27.4 to 5.4 pp. Baselines: 4% before training, 91% with the fact in the prompt. [AUTHOR, PREPRINT] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11020)
- Priming: learning a new text can make the model misapply it in unrelated contexts; the effect is predictable from the pre-learning probability of key tokens and appears in PaLM-2, Gemma and Llama; two mitigations (stepping-stone augmentation, ignore-k update pruning) cut priming by 50-95% while still learning the fact. 1,320-sample "Outlandish" test set. [AUTHOR] — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09522)
- Doc-to-LoRA (Sakana AI authors, ICML 2026): a hypernetwork produces a LoRA from a document in one forward pass (no gradient steps); near-perfect needle-in-a-haystack accuracy at more than 4x the base model's native context; claims to beat standard context distillation on real-world QA but the abstract gives no figures; base model in the ICML slides was Gemma-2-2b-it. Vendor-lab claim, no head-to-head numbers against RAG seen. [AUTHOR] — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15902), [ICML 2026 page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/charakorn26a.html), [project page](https://sakana.ai/doc-to-lora/)

### Inferences
- Rough number for "how many examples": every study that measures it needs a few dozen varied renderings per fact (10 paraphrases in Ovadia; 30 in the reversal-curse run; 24 varied in O'Neill; up to 40 in the low-resource study; roughly 350x token inflation in EntiGraph, 455M/1.3M) plus multiple epochs (up to 5 in Ovadia; 100-1000 exposures in Physics 3.3). A single "NPC heard X" event stored once or twice should be expected to fail.
- Even the best weight-injection recipes land at or just below RAG on the same questions (PD 86.1 vs RAG 86.3 on Amazon; EntiGraph 56.2 vs RAG 60.4; Ovadia FT-par 0.588 vs RAG 0.875), and the strongest ones need generated question sets and hours of GPU per small corpus. Neither study measured update latency of seconds.
- Directional and compositional recall is the weak spot: "Who told you that?" or "What did X say about Y?" are inverse/compositional queries, exactly what reversal-curse and Physics-3.2 results show weights handle worst (0.1%, about 0%) unless training explicitly contains those question forms.
- Combining RAG with fine-tuning did not reliably help beyond RAG in Ovadia, but PD+RAG added 1-3 points in the self-distillation paper; the "both" option is a small gain for large machinery.

### Gaps
- No study I found fine-tunes a quantised 12B-class model on conversation-style episodic memories ("player said X to NPC A on day 3") and compares recall with retrieval; all facts are encyclopaedic or synthetic biographies.
- No exact epochs-vs-recall curves from Ovadia (Figure 4 only) or the low-resource paper (figures only).
- Gekhman's model size unresolved (PaLM 2-S vs -M).
- I did not open the Mecklenburg et al. full text, so I have no numbers from it.

## Knowledge editing (ROME, MEMIT, GRACE, WISE, AlphaEdit and successors): sequential edits before degradation, locality/generalisation, multi-hop failures, fit for thousands of small, changing memories

### Takeaway
Classic locate-and-edit methods (ROME, MEMIT) degrade or collapse after roughly 100-4,000 sequential edits and fail multi-hop questions (multi-hop accuracy falls to about 7% from a 30-40% baseline); newer methods (AlphaEdit, WISE, UltraEdit) report thousands to a million edits with good scores on synthetic benchmarks, but a 2025 re-evaluation under realistic generation finds success rates far below the published ones. Editing is designed for single (subject, relation, object) triples and has not been shown on conversational memories.

### Cited Findings
- Gupta et al., "Model Editing at Scale leads to Gradual and Catastrophic Forgetting": ROME's catastrophic collapse appears between about 100 and 1000 sequential edits on GPT-J (varies by sample); MEMIT declines steeply before 1500 edits on GPT-J and around 4000 on GPT2-XL, catastrophic forgetting after about 1400 edits on GPT-J; MEND loses almost all prior edits and downstream F1 before 100 edits; gradual forgetting of neighbouring facts starts from the first edits; ROME "disabling edits" show about 3 orders of magnitude larger weight distance (3.3e-4 vs 8.2e-7). [INDEP] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07453)
- WISE (side-memory editing; LLaMA-2-7B, ZsRE; reliability / generalisation / locality): T=1: WISE 0.98/0.92/1.00, MEMIT 0.84/0.81/0.99; T=100: WISE 0.90/0.81/1.00, ROME 0.23/0.22/0.04, MEMIT 0.02/0.02/0.02; T=1000: WISE 0.77/0.72/1.00, GRACE 0.93/0.08/1.00, ROME 0.01, MEMIT 0.04; T=3000: WISE-Merge 0.58/0.56/1.00, GRACE 0.96/0.03/1.00, MEMIT-MASS 0.58/0.53/0.47. Hallucination edits at T=600: ROME perplexity 104.93 with locality 0.02, WISE 3.12 / 0.99. The paper itself says reliability, generalisation and locality "can not be realized together" in lifelong editing ("impossible triangle"). WISE-Merge adds 0.64% parameters, about 4% VRAM and about 3% inference delay; the side memory "can be discarded". [AUTHOR] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14768)
- GRACE (codebook of edited activations) reaches high reliability (0.93-0.96) at T=1000-3000 but generalisation to paraphrases only 0.36 at T=1 and 0.03-0.08 at T=1000-3000, i.e. it behaves as an exact-match lookup. — [WISE tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14768)
- AlphaEdit (null-space projection; 2,000 sequential edits in batches of 100; LLaMA3-8B, GPT-J, GPT2-XL; CounterFact and ZsRE): LLaMA3 CounterFact efficacy/generalisation/specificity 98.90/94.22/67.88 vs MEMIT 65.65/64.65/51.56; LLaMA3 ZsRE 94.47/91.13/32.55 vs MEMIT 34.62/31.28/18.49; authors claim an average +36.7% over baseline methods when added to them; limitation stated: multimodal and reasoning models untested. ZsRE "specificity" is 25-33 for every method, i.e. locality is weak there. [AUTHOR; venue not visible in the fetched page, arXiv v4 Apr 2025] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02355)
- UltraEdit (training-free, claims 1M edits; ZsRE, FEVER, WikiBigEdit, own UltraEditBench of 2M+ pairs generated with GPT-4o-mini and only spot-checked): LLaMA-3-8B ZsRE at 20K edits efficacy/generalisation/specificity 90.07/87.36/49.51 vs AlphaEdit 74.34/67.85/22.94, WISE 40.94/40.27/37.40, MEMIT 0.14; at 100K edits 87.80/85.48/46.74; on Mistral-7B AlphaEdit scores 0.00 at 20K; 1M edits on UltraEditBench GPT-J 81.70/76.78/74.96; claims >7x faster and <1/3 VRAM of prior fastest method, editing a 7B model on a 24 GB GPU; RLEdit beats it on LLaMA-3 ZsRE (91.34 vs 90.07). [AUTHOR, PREPRINT; competitor numbers for AlphaEdit/WISE are produced by the rival's authors] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14679v1)
- "The Mirage of Model Editing": re-evaluating with real generation instead of teacher-forcing, average single-edit success is 38.5% vs 96.8% reported previously; Llama-2-7b-chat on QAEdit: ROME 95.5% -> 58.5%, MEMIT 92.9% -> 55.2%, GRACE 98.3% -> 1.2%, WISE 99.8% -> 21.6%; sequential editing, 1000 edits at batch size 1: all methods except FT-M reach about 9.3% average reliability (FT-M 40.5%) with locality 21.3%; with mini-batches no method exceeds 30% at 1000 edits. AlphaEdit and UltraEdit were not in the tables I read. [INDEP] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11177)
- MQuAKE (multi-hop questions after edits), GPT-J, up to 4 edits per instance: MEMIT edit-wise success 96.2% but multi-hop accuracy 7.0% (unedited base 40.5%); ROME 88.3% / 7.4%; MEND 8.0%; Vicuna-7B base 30.2% -> ROME 6.9%, MEMIT 4.9%. With many simultaneous edits (MQuAKE-t, k=1868) MEMIT multi-hop accuracy 0.0%, MEND 4.6%, whereas the memory-based prompting method MeLLo (frozen model, edits in an external store) keeps 30.7% (GPT-J), 51.3% (Vicuna-7B), 85.5% (GPT-3.5); on MQuAKE-cf with 3000 edits MeLLo GPT-3.5 30.9% vs MEMIT GPT-J 5.4%. [AUTHOR] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14795)

### Inferences
- Editing is a poor fit for the NPC use case: it assumes a clean (subject, relation, object) fact with an identifiable subject token, evaluates on Wikipedia-style triples, and (MQuAKE) the edited fact is the one least usable for follow-up reasoning. "Player A told me B is a thief, but C said otherwise" is neither a triple nor stable.
- The lifelong-editing methods that do hold up (WISE side memory, GRACE codebook, MeLLo) work because they add an external or separable store, i.e. they converge on retrieval-like architecture; WISE's own "impossible triangle" statement supports that reading.
- All 1M-edit claims are author-run, on synthetic QA with teacher-forced or template metrics and a generated benchmark; given the Mirage gap (96.8% -> 38.5%) treat headline numbers as upper bounds.

### Gaps
- No independent re-run of AlphaEdit, UltraEdit or RLEdit under the Mirage "wild" protocol in the sources I read.
- I did not find editing evaluated on conversational/episodic memories, belief changes, or with provenance.
- AlphaEdit's published venue was not visible in the fetched page.

## Continual learning and forgetting: sequential fine-tunes, sparse memory finetuning, replay, daily or per-conversation updates

### Takeaway
Sequentially writing facts into weights loses earlier facts and general ability unless the architecture is changed (Meta's sparse memory layers cut forgetting from 71-89% to 11% in a 1.3B model); with ordinary LoRA, retention of earlier facts after only 20 writes ranged from 1% to 46% depending on how each fact was taught, and general benchmark accuracy fell from 81% to 62%.

### Cited Findings
- Sparse memory finetuning (Lin et al., Meta/Berkeley; arXiv Oct 2025): 1.3B model with one FFN layer (layer 12 of 22) replaced by a 1M-slot memory layer (k=32 accesses/token), updating only the top-t slots (t about 100-500; 500 for facts, 10,000 for document QA) ranked by TF-IDF against background accesses. Learning 1K TriviaQA facts sequentially: drop in Natural Questions F1 of 89% (full fine-tuning), 71% (LoRA) and 11% (sparse memory finetuning) at matched new-knowledge acquisition. Limitations stated by the authors: only factual QA, only a 1.3B model, batch-level ranking, optimiser interaction (SGD used for the sparse method, AdamW for baselines). [AUTHOR, PREPRINT] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15103)
- O'Neill et al. (preprint v3, Oct 2026), Qwen3-4B, LoRA r16, 20 sequential writes with merge after each: strict accuracy on earlier facts 1% (repeated statements) vs 46% (24 varied examples); replication on a second fact set 2.9% vs 36.8%. Method comparison after write 20 (varied prompts): SFT 46%, on-policy reverse-KL distillation 31%, on-policy forward-KL 27%, off-policy distillation 13%. O-LoRA orthogonality penalty did not improve retention (44.4% / 46.7% / 45.6% for none / O-LoRA / merged LoRA). Of 327 wrong answers 68.8% contained a later-written fact. General benchmark accuracy fell from 81.0% to 62.3% after 20 varied writes. Facts conflicted with pretrained knowledge; replay/data mixing and retrieval were not studied. [AUTHOR, PREPRINT] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11020)
- LoRA forgets less than full fine-tuning in the source-domain sense: code CPT 20B tokens, average of HellaSwag/ARC-C/WinoGrande 0.545 (full FT) vs 0.617-0.635 (LoRA); but it also learns less (see first section). [INDEP] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09673)
- Self-distillation paper's forgetting check: MMLU-Pro (mean of 6 subjects) Llama-3-8B-Instruct 38.3; plain SFT on the new documents 31.9 (below the 32.7 of the non-instruct base); prompt distillation 34.0, with a Tulu-3 regulariser 37.2. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14964)
- Time-aware evaluation (single-author preprint, Sept 2026): over a 24-month Wikidata stream on Qwen2.5-1.5B, a periodic-update hierarchy's 5.0-point advantage over rank-8 cumulative-replay LoRA becomes an 11.6-point deficit against rank-72 replay; at high rank time-averaged replay leads by 9-13 points. Conclusion: method rankings depend on when and with how much LoRA capacity you measure. [PREPRINT, single author] — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03900)
- Snippet-level 2026 items (not opened): CoRSA reports standard-LoRA forgetting rates of 55.28% and 46.13% for baselines in knowledge revision; RING (Retrieval-Internalized Generation) reports accuracy above 30% under sequential injection with a mixture-of-memory-experts model trained with RL. [SECONDARY] — [search snippets incl. CoRSA arXiv 2602.03696](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.03696), [RING abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01630)
- Cost anchors: one third-party guide estimates QLoRA on a 14B model on an RTX 3090 with Unsloth at about 14-16 GB VRAM and 300-400 tokens/s (estimate, not a benchmark; another vendor table lists 8.5 GB for a 14B 4-bit QLoRA); the self-distillation paper's 8B run took about 24 h on one GPU; Doc-to-LoRA removes gradient steps entirely at the price of a pretrained hypernetwork. — [guide](https://www.stack-junkie.com/blog/lora-fine-tuning-consumer-gpu), [Vast.ai table](https://docs.vast.ai/examples/ai-ml-frameworks/unsloth-studio), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14964), [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15902)

### Inferences
- Updating several times per conversation is the regime where weight memory is weakest: each later write overwrote earlier facts (68.8% of errors in O'Neill) and the only architecture-level fix that works (memory layers with sparse updates) is not available for an off-the-shelf Gemma checkpoint.
- Back-of-envelope (my arithmetic, assumptions: 24 variants of about 60 tokens per fact, 300-400 tok/s for a 12B-class QLoRA, 3-5 epochs): about 1.5k tokens per fact per epoch, i.e. roughly 15-75 s of GPU per fact. That is feasible in the background but competes with serving the same RTX 3090.
- "Forgotten" in weights is often "inaccessible", not erased (see unlearning section), so measuring forgetting by behaviour understates what is still present.

### Gaps
- No paper measured per-conversation or daily adapter updates on a 12B-class quantised model with several events per session over weeks.
- No numbers for replay with episodic fact streams from the 2026 sources I opened (O'Neill did not test it).
- The CoRSA and RING figures were not verified in the papers.
- No sourced tokens/s for Gemma-class 12B on a 3090.

## Unlearning and rollback: removing a trained-in memory vs reverting to an older adapter

### Takeaway
Approximate unlearning is unreliable: the "removed" knowledge can be recovered by brief fine-tuning on loosely related text, LoRA-based unlearning is the easiest to reverse, and quantisation can bring it back. Reverting to an earlier adapter file is exact for a whole-state rollback, but there is no published method for removing a single event while keeping later ones.

### Cited Findings
- Hu et al. (CMU; arXiv v4 Mar 2025, no venue visible): gradient ascent, gradient difference, KL, NPO and SCRUB unlearning can all be undone by "benign relearning" on public text that does not contain the target. WMDP, zephyr-7b-beta, LoRA unlearning, LLM-judge score 1-10 (original 5.92): GA 1.67 -> 5.2, GD 1.09 -> 5.2, NPO 1.0 -> 5.08 after relearning. Harry Potter task: attack success above 70% when unlearning was shallow; "Hermione Granger" recovered from text that excluded it; synthetic study: 100% attack success with text ending in the target name vs 0% with none and 9% with gibberish. The paper says LoRA-based unlearning was especially easy to reverse. [INDEP] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13356)
- 2026 preprints (snippet-level, not opened): DurableUn reports that INT4 quantisation (NF4+LoRA setting) can restore forgotten content up to 22x even when the model passes audits in BF16 and that no tested method achieved strong forgetting, utility and quantisation robustness at once; "Crossing the Margin Cliff" and "RepSelect" propose relearn-robust unlearning and report lower post-attack scores against LoRA attackers. [SECONDARY, PREPRINT] — [DurableUn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02196), [Margin Cliff](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27836), [RepSelect](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.17168)
- Behavioural forgetting is not erasure: in O'Neill et al., of 54 repeated-statement fact-seed pairs that failed every question, the canonical statement kept a median 69% of its likelihood gain; only 3 varied-example pairs met the failure criterion (single log-probability per fact, so tentative). [AUTHOR, PREPRINT] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11020)
- Storage for snapshots: for FlanT5-XXL a full per-user model is about 45 GB while a rank-8 LoRA adapter is about 55 MB. [SECONDARY via ar5iv snippet] — [search result for Salemi & Zamani](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.09510)
- WISE states its modular side memory "can be discarded if edits are harmful" (a discard-the-module rollback, not a per-fact one). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14768)

### Inferences
- Rollback on "load save" is an engineering property, not a research result: if every save slot stores the adapter file (tens of MB per rank-8 adapter on an 11B model, by the Salemi figure) and the base weights are unchanged, restoring it returns exactly the earlier state. This needs deterministic, versioned adapter files and the same quantised base; it is not demonstrated in any paper I found.
- The hard cases are not whole-state rollbacks but "forget that one lie" or "the player retracts X": the evidence says unlearning is shallow and reversible, so the safe design is to retrain from the last good snapshot without the event, or keep per-event adapters, both of which cost GPU time and, for per-event adapters, composition quality that no source measured.
- A quantised base (the user's setup) is specifically flagged by the DurableUn snippet as a risk for unlearning claims; if that is confirmed, "audited at BF16" tests would not transfer.

### Gaps
- No paper on exact reversibility of LoRA training across quantised inference (determinism, merge round-off).
- No evidence for removing one fact from an adapter while preserving later facts; the 2026 unlearning papers were seen only as snippets.
- I found no source on how many adapter snapshots per save slot are practical in a game; sizes are inferred from one FlanT5 figure.

## Provenance and false beliefs in weights: storing belief plus source, training on contradictory or false facts

### Takeaway
Weights can hold a false belief robustly (synthetic document finetuning), and models can learn to cite a source ID, but attribution is weak (top-1 attribution 40-47% even on a 5.7M-token synthetic corpus, with an 80% data blow-up), and I found no work that stores "A believes P because B said so, and B may be lying" in weights.

### Cited Findings
- Source-aware training (TinyLlama 1.1B; BioCite synthetic biographies, 100K documents, 408K facts, 5.7M tokens; each document gets an ID that the model learns to cite): out-of-distribution answer exact match 88.8 with last-name IDs repeated in the document and 95.9 with random 8-digit IDs; attribution Hits@1 only 47.3 and 39.8 respectively. Only "repeat" and "doc-end + chain-of-thought" give meaningful attribution; document-level (not fact-level) augmentation is required; "repeat" adds about 80% pretraining tokens and hurts Wikitext perplexity; doc-begin degrades QA. Authors list synthetic data and small scale as limitations. [AUTHOR] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01019)
- Synthetic document finetuning (Anthropic Alignment Science, Llama 70B Instruct, 24 false facts in four plausibility classes, also Qwen3-32B): SDF-implanted false facts generalise to indirectly related questions (Fermi estimates), stay robust under self-critique and adversarial debate, and an SDF Qwen3-32B still asserts a false cake-baking temperature after nearly 1,000 thinking tokens, whereas prompted beliefs collapse; system prompting and mechanistic editing (AlphaEdit) fail to implant deep beliefs; standard truth probes label implanted falsehoods as true (>95% error for plausible facts), adversarial probes catch most except highly plausible ones; egregiously false facts remain brittle. Blog post gives no exact percentages in text, only figures; authors note facts were simple and only factual (not values) beliefs were tested. [AUTHOR, not peer reviewed (blog; arXiv version listed as 2510.17941)] — [Alignment Science blog](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/believe-it-or-not), [secondary summary](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/believe-it-or-not-how-deeply-do-llms-believe-implanted-facts)
- Implicit meta-learning (Krasheninnikov et al., preprint v4 Jul 2024): random-string "tags" marking text as useful make a fine-tuned model shift later updates toward tagged text and internalise it more; stronger in larger models and with smaller batches; source reliability is the framing, but my fetch showed no reliable-vs-unreliable experiment numbers. [AUTHOR, PREPRINT] — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15047); the claim that this is the reference for "trust more reliable sources" is from [Evans' OOCR primer](https://owainevans.github.io/ocr-primer.html) via a search snippet
- Knowledge conflicts, in-context only: a Jan 2026 paper (title "Whose Facts Win?" per the search summary; the URL mapping below is my guess from the result list) reports LLMs rank source credibility (government > newspapers > individuals > social media) and that repetition from a low-credibility source can flip the preference. [SECONDARY, unopened] — [arXiv 2601.03746](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.03746v1)
- Leakage of new facts: priming results (see first section) show a newly learned fact can surface in unrelated contexts, predictable from token probabilities, mitigation reduces it 50-95%. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09522)
- Hallucination on unfamiliar facts: Gekhman et al. show that fitting facts the model did not know increases hallucination roughly linearly. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05904)
- Inverse questions ("who said X?") are the failure mode in weights: about 0% inverse search (Physics 3.2), 0.1% reverse recall (reversal curse). — [Physics 3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14402), [reversal curse](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.12288)

### Inferences
- Because SDF-style training makes a belief behave like a first-class fact and defend itself, a lie trained into an NPC's weights would not stay marked as "I was told this"; the belief-plus-source link would have to be trained as an additional cited-ID behaviour, which in the only study measuring it recovers the exact source in less than half of cases.
- "Who said what" requires both forward (statement -> speaker) and inverse (speaker -> statements) recall plus provenance; the sources show weights do forward recall only with heavy augmentation and inverse recall essentially not at all.
- Contradictory information about the same entity (NPC told two stories) is predicted by O'Neill's results to resolve by recency/interference (later writes overwrite earlier ones), not by tracked belief revision.

### Gaps
- No paper found that trains on contradictory claims from labelled speakers and measures belief revision with provenance.
- The Anthropic result has no exact numbers in the text I read; the arXiv version was not opened.
- "Whose Facts Win?" and the implicit-meta-learning reliability experiment were not verified at paper level.

## Personalisation evidence: RAG vs per-user PEFT (Salemi & Zamani, OPPU) and later comparisons

### Takeaway
On the LaMP benchmark, retrieval gives most of the personalisation gain (+14.92% average over the non-personalised model vs +1.07% for per-user LoRA), the combination adds a small amount (+15.98%), and per-user PEFT starts to pay off only with large user histories; OPPU's authors report larger PEFT wins with a different setup. None of this measures memorising discrete facts.

### Cited Findings
- Salemi & Zamani (ICTIR '25), FlanT5-XXL (11B), LaMP's seven tasks, k=4 retrieved items (BM25/Contriever/Recency/RSPG), LoRA rank 8-64, 50 epochs: average improvement over the non-personalised LLM RAG 14.92%, PEFT 1.07%, PEFT+RAG 15.98% (0.44% relative above RAG; better than RAG on 4 of 7 tasks). PEFT gains correlate positively with profile size on 5 of 7 datasets, RAG's gain correlates negatively; authors conclude RAG for cold-start users, PEFT when much data exists. Largest PEFT gain on LaMP-6 (email subject), attributed to private data absent from pretraining. [INDEP of OPPU] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09510); venue from the PDF metadata via [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.09510)
- OPPU "One PEFT Per User" (Llama-2-7B, 100 most active users, LaMP; per-user LoRA, BM25 retrieval): every task improves with OPPU variants, e.g. LaMP-3 product rating MAE 0.214 -> 0.143 and RMSE 0.463 -> 0.378, LaMP-1 accuracy 0.756 -> 0.797, but LaMP-4 headline R-1 only 0.198 -> 0.199; OPPU plus retrieval gives +1.93% relative over OPPU without retrieval and +2.48% over retrieval without OPPU; when the 100 history items most similar to the query are removed, retrieval-only drops toward the non-personalised baseline while OPPU is "more robust" (exact numbers not extracted); training 100 adapters took 12 minutes to 12 hours on 3 A6000s. [AUTHOR] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04401)
- Later hypernetwork direction: user-profile -> LoRA generators (Personalized Pieces, P2P) and a 2026 paper reporting gains over RAG on LaMP ("Beyond Retrieval: Learning Compact User Representations", unopened). [SECONDARY] — [Multi-LoRA overview](https://lin-guanguo.github.io/llm-memory-research/multi-lora.research/), [arXiv 2606.04547](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04547)
- External-memory baseline for conversational facts (ConvoMem, Salesforce AI Research, preprint Nov 2025; 75,336 QA pairs, 2-300 conversations of history; author-built benchmark and author-run comparison, no fine-tuning arm): long-context stuffing about 82% overall at the start declining to about 65% at 300 conversations (user facts about 95% -> 75-83%); Mem0 (RAG-style memory) 77.5% vs 94.7% for long context on user facts and 30-45% vs 77-90% on preferences; paper's guidance: long context best for roughly the first 30 conversations, workable to about 150, hybrid/RAG after. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10523v1)
- LongMemEval (ICLR 2025; 500 questions): a retrieval/long-context benchmark; one blog reports an 11.8-point gap (72.4% vs 60.6%) between its S and M settings. It does not evaluate parametric memory. [SECONDARY for the 11.8 pts] — [ICLR page](https://www.iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/28290), [Couchbase write-up](https://www.couchbase.com/blog/what-we-learned-evaluating-agent-memorythe-setup-part-1/)

### Inferences
- The two LaMP studies disagree on the size of the PEFT benefit (small in Salemi & Zamani, larger in OPPU) but differ in base model (FlanT5-XXL vs Llama-2-7B), user sampling (all vs 100 most active) and metrics; both agree that RAG+PEFT is at least as good as either alone.
- LaMP tasks are style/preference/classification personalisation (citations, ratings, headlines). They reward absorbing a user's habits from hundreds of items, which suits LoRA, and say little about whether a discrete event heard once is recalled.
- For an NPC with tens to a few hundred episodic items, ConvoMem's pattern (small memories fit in context) is the relevant external baseline; the "long context beats RAG" caveat comes from an author-built benchmark.

### Gaps
- No paper compares per-user PEFT and retrieval on discrete, frequently changing user facts with updates between sessions.
- OPPU's venue and exact behaviour-shift numbers were not extractable; its storage cost per user is not reported in the paper.
- The 2026 hypernetwork personalisation results are unverified.

## Synthesis for the NPC-memory question: would a small model trained in the background recall events, who said what, and beliefs reliably; cost and risk

### Takeaway
On current evidence the answer is no for the facts that matter to gameplay: weight-trained memory would recall the exact event only after dozens of varied rewrites and several epochs per fact, would not reliably answer "who said X", lose older items as new ones are written, and cannot be edited one fact at a time or tagged with a source; an external, structured store (with a retrieval or in-context step) is better supported by every comparison I found, and weights are best treated as an optional, lossy style/familiarity layer.

### Cited Findings
- Recall vs RAG: fine-tuned 0.504-0.588 vs RAG 0.875 on post-cutoff news (Mistral-7B) — [Ovadia et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05934); the best weight-injection recipe roughly ties RAG after about 24 GPU-hours per small corpus — [self-distillation](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14964).
- Update survival: 1% (two formats per fact) to 46% (24 varied examples) of earlier facts retained after 20 sequential LoRA writes, with general accuracy 81.0% -> 62.3% — [O'Neill et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11020).
- Inverse/relational recall: 0.1% reverse accuracy after 30 paraphrases per fact — [reversal curse](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.12288).
- Editing at scale: realistic single-edit success 38.5% (vs 96.8% claimed) and 9.3% average reliability after 1000 sequential single edits — [Mirage](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11177).
- Unlearning: relearning from unrelated text restores unlearned knowledge (score 1.67 -> 5.2 vs original 5.92 under LoRA unlearning) — [Hu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13356).
- Provenance: 47.3% top-1 attribution in a 1.1B model on synthetic biographies — [source-aware training](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01019).
- Personalisation: RAG +14.92% vs per-user PEFT +1.07% over a non-personalised model; both +15.98% — [Salemi & Zamani](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09510).

### Inferences
- Reliability: what weights do well is repeated, stable, forward-direction content (a character's speech habits, standing relationships, well-rehearsed backstory); what the NPC design needs (events that change several times per conversation, retractions, lies, speaker attribution, exact rollback) is where every cited method is weakest.
- Cost: a background LoRA pass is feasible on one RTX 3090 (QLoRA throughput of about 300-400 tok/s for a 14B per one guide), but each fact needs tens of generated variants (an extra LLM step) and the GPU is shared with the serving model; this adds latency to the "memory visible" moment, whereas retrieval is visible on the next turn.
- Risk list (each supported above): recall failures that are silent (a fact is simply absent, no flag); interference/overwriting between events; priming leakage of an event into unrelated dialogue; implanted false beliefs that resist later correction (SDF); no per-belief source; unlearning that does not hold up; quantised-base recovery of "removed" content (snippet-level).
- If the user still wants weights involved, the least-risky evidence-backed shape is: structured store owns truth, provenance and rollback; retrieval feeds the small model; an optional per-character LoRA trained from the store's contents (not the other way round) supplies style and familiarity, with every snapshot saved per save slot so a load restores it exactly. Doc-to-LoRA-like hypernetworks or self-distillation could later compile store contents into an adapter, but no evidence shows they beat just reading the store.
- A test that would settle it for this project: take the existing memory graph, compile 50-200 events (including lies and retractions) into a LoRA by the O'Neill varied-example recipe, and measure recall of the exact event, speaker attribution, and the false-belief flag against retrieval on the same Gemma 12B. Nothing published covers it.

### Gaps
- No direct experiment matches the NPC scenario; every number is by analogy from encyclopaedic, news, synthetic-biography or LaMP data.
- Several 2026 papers (CoRSA, RING, DurableUn, Margin Cliff, RepSelect, "Whose Facts Win?", Beyond Retrieval) were seen only as search snippets and should be opened before being cited.
- Gemma-family specifics: Gemma appears only in the priming paper's model list and in UltraEdit's Gemma-3-27B scalability runs; nothing measures Gemma 12B fine-tuning on facts.
- Source-quality summary: independent results are Gupta et al. (editing), Mirage (editing), Hu et al. (unlearning), Salemi & Zamani (vs OPPU), LoRA-Learns-Less (LoRA behaviour); the rest are author claims, several are single preprints with no visible venue (sparse memory finetuning, O'Neill et al., UltraEdit, self-distillation, low-resource injection, implicit meta-learning, single-author time-aware evaluation) and the Anthropic SDF result is a lab blog post.
